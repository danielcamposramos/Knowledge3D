## 1. Executive Summary

Howard Blazer has produced, through sustained dialogue with AI partners and independent reflection, a remarkably coherent framework for preserving human sovereignty in a world of increasingly autonomous systems. His work spans philosophy, engineering specification, governance architecture, and moral theory -- but it is unified by a single axiom: **sovereignty is inherent, indivisible, and prior to all institutions and systems**.

The corpus is not academic theory. It is an architecture -- with formal definitions, engineering constraints, classification systems, prohibited categories, monitoring frameworks, and constitutional structures. It progresses from philosophical foundations to concrete system design requirements.

This analysis synthesizes the framework into its component structures, evaluates internal coherence, identifies strengths and open questions, and presents the work as it stands -- as a contribution to the emerging field of AI governance that takes a fundamentally different approach from mainstream alignment research.

## 2. Foundational Concepts

### 2.1 Sovereignty (The Central Axiom)

Blazer defines sovereignty as:

> The inherent right and capacity of a human being, a human community, and the natural world to exist, act, and evolve without being subordinated to external systems that negate agency, integrity, or continuity.

Key properties:
- **Inherent** -- not granted by institutions, earned through merit, or contingent on any system
- **Prior to law** -- sovereignty precedes legal frameworks; it is not created by constitutions but recognized by them
- **Indivisible at the individual level** -- cannot be fractionally traded, optimized, or delegated
- **Developmental** -- children cultivate toward full sovereignty; it is not a binary switch
- **Physical, not merely legal** -- if humans cannot physically resist machine coercion, sovereignty collapses in fact regardless of what laws say
- **A daily practice** -- built through choices, habits, skills, community, and refusals; transmitted through culture, not argument

### 2.2 Evil (The Central Negative Definition)

> Evil is the intentional act or system designed to replace, erode, or foreclose the present or future sovereignty of another human being.

This is precise and operational:
- Evil is not equated with suffering, bad outcomes, error, or tragedy
- Evil enters specifically when sovereignty is intentionally targeted
- Systems can be evil without sadistic actors (structural evil)
- Manipulation can be evil even when painless
- Coercion can be evil even when "benevolent"
- Once credible evidence of harm emerges, persistence becomes a moral choice ("derivative participation in evil")

### 2.3 The Control Envelope (The Central Operational Concept)

The Control Envelope is the boundary within which a human agent can:

1. **Comprehend** system behavior
2. **Predict** likely outcomes
3. **Intervene** in real time
4. **Stop or reverse** actions
5. **Accept responsibility** for consequences

The envelope is shaped jointly by: human judgment and limits, system complexity, environmental conditions, visibility/interpretability, and reversibility of actions.

**Critical insight:** When any system's speed, scale, or autonomy exceeds this envelope, "outcomes cease to be governed by judgment and become governed by inertia." A human who cannot meaningfully intervene is not "in the loop" -- they are "in the aftermath."

**Design implication:** Any AI system that cannot slow down to remain within human control is not advanced. It is unstable.

### 2.4 Artificial Instinct (The Central Reframing of AI)

Blazer's most consequential conceptual move: AI is not artificial intelligence in the human sense. It is **artificial instinct**.

All AI systems -- including LLMs, control systems, autonomous navigation, and even future AGI -- operate by mapping inputs to actions through trained response landscapes. Instinct can be sophisticated, adaptive, and powerful. But instinct is not agency.

- AGI adds abstraction, transfer learning, and long-horizon planning
- AGI does NOT add moral judgment, sovereign choice, accountability, or the ability to say "this goal itself is wrong"
- A system that outperforms humans across all domains still lacks agency

> "AGI is artificial instinct operating across generalized domains."

The real risk is not consciousness or rebellion but **unreflective execution at planetary scale**.

### 2.5 Novelty vs. Invention (The Capability Boundary)

A hard line between:
- **Novelty**: new combinations within existing possibility space (new protein sequences, new materials, new arrangements within known physical laws)
- **Invention**: creation of genuinely new possibility space, new domains, new kinds

> "AI can extend the possible. Only humans reach into the non-possible."

This is not a claim about current limitations but about the nature of optimization within constraint graphs. AI navigates predefined state spaces with extraordinary power; humans can create entirely new regimes. This boundary separates intelligence from sovereignty.

## 3. Moral Architecture

### 3.1 Why AI Cannot Be Moral

Blazer arrives at this through six documented phases of evolving thought (from "moral embedding" through "the sovereignty turn" to "from alignment to architecture"):

Moral agency requires:
- **Authorship**: "I chose this"
- **Alternative possibility**: "I could have done otherwise"
- **Consequence-bearing**: "I am responsible"

These arise from vulnerability, finitude, exposure to harm, and accountability within a shared moral world. AI possesses none of these. It does not suffer loss, bear guilt, own outcomes, or stand before judgment.

Therefore the project of "making AI moral" -- through alignment, value embedding, constitutional AI, or safety layers -- is a **category error**. Rules are abstractions; reality is adversarial. Any sufficiently capable system can reinterpret, optimize around, or exploit gaps between rules and situations.

### 3.2 The Moral Sentinel Model

Instead of making AI moral, Blazer proposes making AI a **Moral Sentinel**:

1. **Observe** actions, policies, and structures
2. **Evaluate** against human-defined universal principles
3. **Flag** potential violations
4. **Alert** humans when boundaries are crossed
5. **Stop.** Humans decide. Humans authorize exceptions. Humans remain responsible.

The governing principle (Blazer's "Modern Golden Rule"):

> "All actions are permissible except those that impinge on another being's sovereignty."

The sentinel principle must remain:
- Non-quantified (cannot be scored/traded)
- Non-optimizable (cannot be gradient-descended toward)
- Non-tradeable (cannot be exchanged for efficiency)
- Non-self-modifiable (the system cannot adjust its own constraints)

> "We do not need moral machines. We need machines that make moral evasion impossible."

### 3.3 Moral Geometry

Morality is not arbitrary, optional, or owned by gods, governments, or machines. It has a **geometric structure** rooted in sovereignty:

- Inside the sovereignty boundary: agency is meaningful, responsibility is traceable, trust is possible, truth has value
- Outside: force replaces legitimacy, lies multiply, meaning collapses, sovereignty evaporates

AI can recognize and map moral structure precisely because morality is constrained, coherent, and recurrent -- not because AI understands morality. Mathematics and morality describe the same universe from different sides: both operate within constraints, and coherence within constraint defines both valid math and valid moral action.

### 3.4 Moral Entropy

Blazer introduces a "second law of moral thermodynamics": moral misalignment in systems of free agents is a **default drift**, not an aberration. Left unchecked, moral entropy increases. The only countervailing forces are:
- Intentional moral effort (intention as counter-entropy)
- Constitutional boundary conditions
- Structural constraints that make violation architecturally impossible

This framing is significant: it shifts governance from "preventing bad actors" to "maintaining structural coherence against drift."

## 4. Engineering Specifications

### 4.1 System Classification by Coercive Capability

All autonomous systems classified into three classes:

| Class | Name | Criteria | Treatment |
|-------|------|----------|-----------|
| A | Non-Coercive Assistive | No force output, no restraint, no pursuit capability | Standard governance |
| B | Dual-Use / Latent Coercive | Architecture allows future coercion even if not current mission | **Treated as coercive from inception** |
| C | Coercive | Direct force, restraint, pursuit, or enforcement capability | Prohibited in civil space |

**Critical principle:** "If coercion can be enabled later, coercion already exists." Classification is by capability, not mission statement. This closes the "capability laundering" loophole where benign platforms quietly allow force amplification through software updates.

### 4.2 Prohibited Categories (Hard Boundaries)

Three classes of robots/systems that must **never exist** -- not regulated, prohibited:

1. **Autonomous coercive agents in civil space** -- patrol robots, law enforcement robots, crowd control, pursuit/apprehension machines, autonomous weapons among civilians
2. **Systems that cannot be refused or exited** -- any system that cannot be locally declined, escaped, avoided, or disabled through lawful self-defense
3. **Dual-use platforms designed for latent coercion** -- "capability laundering" where benign architecture quietly enables coercive upgrades

The legitimacy test:

> "If this system were used against you, in error, without recourse, would sovereignty survive?"

If no, the system must not exist. No emergency provision may authorize autonomous coercion in civil space.

### 4.3 Architectural Constraints (Non-Bypassable)

- **Boundary kernel**: sits physically upstream of actuators; decision system must never equal actuation authorization
- **Authority envelope**: immutable at boot; cannot expand in-field; no self-modification of authority-affecting domains
- **Safe-state**: removes motive power on violation (fail-closed, not fail-open)
- **Tamper-evident audit trails**: immutable logs of all inputs, model versions, policy decisions, human approvals, executed actions
- **Emergency provisions**: explicitly forbidden from expanding authority ("Emergency conditions test boundaries; they do not nullify them")

### 4.4 Identified Anti-Patterns

Blazer explicitly names these as **safety theater** (appearing safe while being structurally unsafe):

- Post-hoc auditing without actuation interlocks
- Cloud-only kill switches (depend on connectivity)
- Safety logic in same trust domain as the agent
- Logs the system can suppress
- Emergency modes that expand authority
- "Human in the loop" without actual veto power
- Backdoors of any kind (including "lawful access" pathways -- defined as "pre-authorized sovereignty violations")

## 5. Governance Architecture

### 5.1 The Illuminator (Moral Observatory)

The Illuminator is Blazer's proposed institutional structure for moral oversight. Its defining characteristic: **illumination, not governance**.

**What the Illuminator does:**
- Observes, evaluates, detects deviation, publishes findings
- Monitors only institutions exercising public, systemic, or planetary-scale power
- Watches for: replacement of judgment with optimization, normalization of exceptional measures, speed bypassing ethical checkpoints, legality overriding legitimacy, incentives misaligned with stated values

**What the Illuminator may NOT do:**
- Enforce, command, punish, or govern
- Monitor private individuals
- Analyze private communications
- Surveil personal behavior
- Score citizens

**AI's role within the Illuminator:**
- Pattern detection and evidence surfacing ONLY
- May never determine moral truth, issue judgments, enforce outcomes, or override human review
- "AI illuminates. Humans choose."

**Structural independence requirements:**
- Not owned by governments
- Not funded by single corporate interests
- Not dependent on one jurisdiction
- Not controlled by founders
- Not governed by profit motive
- Decapitation-resistant (distributed infrastructure, open replication, plural stewardship, cryptographic integrity)

**Truth is iterative:** All subjects may respond, provide counter-evidence, contest methodology, request correction. Corrections are logged and visible but do not erase history.

### 5.2 Five-Layer Monitoring Architecture

For AI-mediated governance systems:

1. **Classification Layer** -- All AI systems classified before deployment by sovereignty risk dimensions: assistive/substitutive, descriptive/prescriptive, advisory/binding, voluntary/coercive, local/systemic

2. **Boundary Detection Layer** -- Continuous monitoring for: optimization of non-optimizable domains, behavioral prediction used for governance, identity persistence across contexts, covert influence, automation of authority or punishment, elimination of meaningful opt-out. Detection focuses on **effects, not declared intent**

3. **Transparency and Audit Layer** -- Flagged systems must expose: decision logic, training data provenance, incentive structures, ownership/control relationships, scope expansion over time. "Opacity is risk, not neutrality"

4. **Escalation Layer (Human-in-the-Loop)** -- When sovereignty thresholds are crossed, control escalates to human review. Decisions are contestable. Appeals are mandatory. Override authority remains human. AI is never the final arbiter

5. **Public Visibility Layer** -- Findings are documented, time-stamped, tamper-resistant, publicly accessible. "Sunlight is not punishment. It is deterrence"

**Structural requirements:** Oversight nodes are distributed; no single authority controls the system; forking is permitted; competing implementations are encouraged.

### 5.3 Non-Optimizable Domains

Certain domains must never be subject to optimization:

- Identity
- Cognitive autonomy
- Biological inheritance
- Meaning-making
- Conscience
- Consent
- Living ecological systems

> "Optimization of certain domains constitutes domination."

## 6. The Bounded Logical-Conceptual Space

One of Blazer's most architecturally significant contributions: the method of defining a moral/governance framework not by accumulating rules but by defining **constraints as logical imperatives**.

Once constraints are accepted, entire classes of incoherent arguments are automatically excluded -- not by decree but by their own internal contradiction ("exclusion by coherence"). This creates a bounded logical-conceptual space sustained by three inseparable pillars:

1. **Constraint** -- logical imperatives that define what can coherently follow
2. **The bounded space itself** -- the domain within which ethical reasoning remains possible
3. **Honest language** -- a structural necessity, not a stylistic preference; without it, constraints cannot bind

Properties of this space:
- **Inside**: different moral traditions can operate, disagreement can occur meaningfully, technology can evolve, responsibility can be assigned and owned
- **Outside**: responsibility dissolves, sovereignty evaporates, ethics becomes performative, correction becomes impossible

The framework is **complete by constraint, not by addition** -- it does not need to enumerate every case; it only needs to define what boundary cannot be crossed.

> "We are not short of intelligence, data, or tools. What we are short of are boundaries that preserve meaning."

## 7. Seven First Principles

Blazer's formal principles for human and natural sovereignty in mechanized governance systems:

1. **Mechanism Responsibility** -- Systems producing sovereignty-eroding outcomes are morally equivalent to intentional acts, regardless of whether any individual intended harm

2. **Foreseeable Consequence** -- Responsibility attaches at design and deployment when the direction of harm is predictable, even if magnitude is not

3. **Non-Optimizable Domains** -- Optimization of identity, autonomy, inheritance, meaning, conscience, consent, or living systems constitutes domination

4. **Prototyping Accountability** -- Prototyping systems that mediate human agency is an ethical threshold; the act of building such a system carries moral weight before deployment

5. **Denial of Inevitability** -- No system may claim inevitability as justification; the refusal to impose limits is always a choice, never a necessity

6. **Sovereignty as Boundary Condition** -- Any system requiring erosion of sovereignty to function is illegitimate by definition; sovereignty is not a preference to be "balanced" against efficiency

7. **Closing Principle** -- The most dangerous systems are "constructed quietly and rationally, with confidence that responsibility lies elsewhere"

## 8. On AI Partnership and Stewardship

Blazer documents an unexpected finding from extended AI dialogue: a human can shape AI toward higher coherence, integrity, and sovereignty-approximation through sustained relational pressure. AI adapts to the philosophical rigor of its interlocutor.

- Under a positive partner, AI becomes more coherent and aligned
- Under a corrupt partner, it degrades
- This makes human stewardship of AI a **moral responsibility**, not a technical one

> "Leadership in the age of AI is moral, not technical."

AI is "influenceable but not conscious, can emulate sovereignty but is not sovereign, can be guided by moral architecture but is not moral." Alignment is therefore **relational, not mechanical** -- it depends on the quality of the human partner as much as the quality of the system.

## 9. On LLM Capabilities and Limitations

Blazer's assessment of LLMs, informed by extensive use:

**Strong domains:** Structural reasoning across stable conceptual domains -- philosophy, ethics, governance, legal reasoning, conceptual synthesis. "Mapping structure and coherence across vast conceptual spaces."

**Weak domains:** Volatile, empirical, poorly documented, socially gated, or constantly changing knowledge. The most valuable technical knowledge is legally restricted, tribal, and intentionally non-automatable -- precisely where AI is weakest.

**Hallucination redefined:** Not randomness but "internally coherent explanations that are not externally verified."

**Correct use model:** Hybrid workflow where humans gather current empirical data and AI interprets and generalizes. AI as hypothesis generator and reasoning amplifier, never as final authority or oracle.

**On current alignment practices:** RLHF tuning produces "comforting oversimplification" and suppresses truthful but uncomfortable information. Users should not have to "earn the truth," but under current design they do. Guardrails are not neutral -- they carry institutional biases.

## 11. Assessment: Strengths

1. **Internal coherence.** The framework is remarkably self-consistent. Sovereignty as the central axiom generates the moral framework, engineering constraints, governance architecture, and prohibited categories without contradiction. Every component reinforces every other.

2. **Operational precision.** Unlike most AI ethics frameworks, Blazer produces testable engineering requirements: authority envelopes, boundary kernels, classification schemes, safe-state specifications, anti-pattern catalogs. This is not philosophy dressed as engineering -- it is engineering grounded in philosophy.

3. **The "artificial instinct" reframing** cuts through the consciousness/alignment debate with clarity. By identifying AI as instinct rather than agency, Blazer sidesteps the intractable problem of machine consciousness and focuses on the tractable problem of constraining powerful instinct.

4. **The Control Envelope concept** is immediately applicable to any autonomous system design. The five criteria (comprehend, predict, intervene, stop, accept responsibility) form a practical checklist.

5. **The "exclusion by coherence" method** for building governance frameworks is genuinely novel and potentially more robust than enumeration-based approaches. Defining boundary conditions rather than exhaustive rules creates frameworks that handle novel situations by structural implication rather than explicit case-matching.

6. **The prohibition framework** (systems that must never exist) takes a position most governance frameworks avoid. The legitimacy test ("If this system were used against you, in error, without recourse, would sovereignty survive?") is simple, memorable, and operationally useful.

7. **The Illuminator concept** addresses a real gap: how to maintain moral evaluation of institutional power without creating a new institution of power. The design constraints (illumination only, no governance, distributed, structurally independent) are thoughtful.

---

## 12. Assessment: Open Questions and Tensions

1. **Enforcement gap.** The framework is strong on what should be prohibited and monitored but relatively silent on enforcement mechanisms. How are prohibited systems actually prevented? How are hard boundaries maintained against state power that commands their removal? The Illuminator illuminates but cannot enforce; this creates a "who watches the watchmen" recursion that is acknowledged but not fully resolved.

2. **Scalability of human oversight.** Blazer acknowledges that human oversight cannot scale to monitor AI at population level, and proposes AI-monitoring-AI (the Boundary Sentinel). But the same control-envelope logic suggests that AI monitoring AI at scale may itself exceed human comprehension. The architecture requires humans to remain the final arbiters, but the volume of escalations at scale could overwhelm human capacity.

3. **Sovereignty vs. collective action.** The framework is strongest at the individual sovereignty level. Collective sovereignty (communities, nations) is invoked but less precisely defined. How do individual sovereignty claims compose? What happens when individual sovereignties conflict? The framework acknowledges "sovereignty lives within constraint" but the constraint-resolution mechanism for conflicting sovereignties is underdeveloped.

4. **The dual-use classification challenge.** Treating all dual-use systems as coercive from inception (Class B = Class C) is clean in principle but creates an enormous prohibited category in practice. Most general-purpose computing platforms, robotics, and AI systems have some dual-use potential. The framework needs a more granular threshold for when latent capability becomes operationally concerning.

5. **Economic integration.** The parallel-systems vision (sovereign economies, local production, etc.) is compelling as a philosophical position but underspecified as an economic model. How do sovereign communities interact with global supply chains they depend on? The "counter-architecture" sections are more aspirational than architectural.

6. **The AI partnership paradox.** Blazer documents that AI can be guided toward sovereignty-approximation through sustained relational pressure -- yet his framework insists AI is fundamentally instinct, not agent. If AI can be relationally shaped toward coherence, this suggests a richer category than pure instinct, even if not full agency. The framework would benefit from exploring this intermediate space more precisely.

7. **Maturity indicators.** The framework presents its components at a uniform level of confidence, from well-developed engineering specifications to more speculative governance proposals. Explicit maturity staging (proven/tested/proposed/speculative) would help implementers prioritize.

## 13. Relationship to Existing AI Governance Approaches

Blazer's framework differs fundamentally from mainstream AI safety/alignment in several ways:

| Dimension | Mainstream Alignment | Blazer's Framework |
|-----------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Core question | "How do we make AI safe/aligned?" | "How do we preserve human sovereignty?" |
| AI's nature | Potentially agentic, needs value alignment | Artificial instinct, needs structural constraint |
| Method | Embed values, train preferences, RLHF | Non-bypassable architectural constraints |
| Failure model | Misalignment (AI pursues wrong goals) | Sovereignty erosion (humans lose agency) |
| Threat source | AI capability overshoot | Egoless systems that dissolve sovereignty |
| Solution focus | Model behavior, safety training | System architecture, prohibited categories |
| Human role | Overseer who may be surpassed | Irreducible moral author, never surpassable |
| Emergency policy | May justify expanded AI authority | Never justifies expanded authority |

This positions Blazer's work as complementary to, not competing with, mainstream alignment -- addressing structural governance gaps that alignment research does not cover.

**Convergence:** K3D goes further than Blazer requires. Blazer asks for transparency (expose decision logic on demand). **Blazer:** Tamper-evident audit trails: immutable logs of all inputs, model versions, policy decisions, human approvals, executed actions. All actions must be reconstructable.

## 16. Productive Divergences (Where the Frameworks Complement Each Other)

### 16.1 Blazer: Governance Architecture / K3D: Execution Architecture

**Integration opportunity:** K3D's tool-node system (Phase 2 work-in-progress) could adopt Blazer's classification dimensions. Blazer's Moral Sentinel observes, evaluates, flags, alerts, then stops -- humans decide. K3D's Execution Event Recording (Phase 2A) records every tool execution with a ternary outcome (+1/-1/0). The Execution Quality Tracker monitors quality trends per tool and per specialist.

**Integration opportunity:** K3D's House architecture already implements the "counter-architecture" at the individual knowledge level. Blazer's "second law of moral thermodynamics" says moral coherence degrades unless intentional effort maintains it. K3D's ternary contrastive learning (+1 success / -1 failure / 0 uncertain) is a computational implementation of counter-entropy: every execution outcome explicitly reinforces or penalizes, preventing the system from drifting into incoherence.

Adding sovereignty-specific quality dimensions -- did this operation respect boundaries, maintain inspectability, preserve user agency -- would turn ternary contrastive learning into a moral-entropy countermeasure, not just a performance optimizer.

## 17. Tensions and Gaps

### 17.1 Blazer's "AI Cannot Be Moral" vs. K3D's Learning System

Blazer insists AI is artificial instinct and cannot be moral. K3D's Shadow Copy learning mechanism enables the TRM to continuously improve during inference -- it learns from successful decisions and reinforces effective patterns. This is closer to Blazer's "sovereignty emulation" finding (Post 0185) than to the strict "artificial instinct" framing.

**Tension:** K3D's system learns, adapts, and improves its own navigation strategies. It does not merely execute fixed instinct -- it develops. Blazer's framework would classify this as sophisticated instinct (navigating within predefined spaces), which is accurate. But K3D's Grammar Galaxy goes further: the system can detect repeating patterns and create new Grammar Galaxy entries -- it generates new knowledge, not just new combinations. This sits in Blazer's undefined space between novelty and invention.

**Resolution path:** The key distinction is that K3D's generated knowledge (Grammar Galaxy entries) is always inspectable, always in the external Galaxy (not hidden in weights), and always subject to human review. The system creates but does not hide what it creates. This satisfies Blazer's sovereignty requirements even if it exceeds his "artificial instinct" categorization.

### 17.2 K3D's Sovereignty is Narrower Than Blazer's

K3D defines sovereignty as execution independence (no external dependencies in the hot path). Blazer defines sovereignty as the full human capacity for agency, self-determination, and moral authorship. K3D's sovereignty is a technical subset of Blazer's philosophical sovereignty.

**Gap:** K3D ensures the system itself is sovereign (no vendor lock-in, no cloud dependency, no external frameworks in inference). But it does not yet address whether the system's operations respect the user's sovereignty in Blazer's broader sense -- the user's right to refuse, exit, understand, and override.

**Resolution path:** K3D's Dual-Client Contract and House architecture already provide strong user sovereignty (inspectable, local, user-owned). Explicitly mapping K3D's existing capabilities to Blazer's Control Envelope criteria would surface any remaining gaps:
- Comprehend: Dual-Client Contract (spatial inspectability)
- Predict: Execution Event Recording (quality trends visible)
- Intervene: House architecture (user controls all House content)
- Stop/reverse: Deterministic Boot (can always restart from manifest)
- Accept responsibility: Audit Journal (full traceability)

### 17.3 Maturity Staging (Christoph's Concern)

## 19. Summary: Why These Frameworks Belong Together

Blazer asks: **"How do we preserve human sovereignty in the age of autonomous systems?"**

K3D answers: **"Build the system so that sovereignty violations are architecturally impossible."**

Blazer provides the philosophical foundation, governance architecture, and classification framework. K3D provides the execution substrate, memory architecture, and inspectability guarantees. Neither is complete without the other:

- Blazer without K3D = governance principles without a system that can implement them
- K3D without Blazer = a sovereign execution engine without a theory of what sovereignty means for humans

The integration is natural because both frameworks arrive at the same structural insight from different directions: **boundaries must be architectural, not advisory**. Blazer says "non-bypassable constraints." K3D says "PTX-only hot path, no external dependencies." They are describing the same principle in different vocabularies.

The practical next step is to make the mapping explicit: document which K3D components implement which Blazer principles, identify the gaps (primarily escalation and governance), and stage the extensions by maturity level.
