# Carbon Projection Methodology

## Objective
Estimate long-horizon carbon impact if procedural memory architectures reduce duplicated data and repeated AI compute across key digital sectors.

## Inputs
The carbon blueprint uses sector-level baselines, growth assumptions, and adoption curves. It models savings from:
- procedural compression and deduplication
- reduced repeated compute for equivalent knowledge operations
- improved reuse of canonical procedural artifacts

## Modeling Structure
1. Baseline emissions context
- Start from published digital and AI energy trend framing in the project carbon blueprint.

2. Adoption curve assumptions
- Early, mid, and broad adoption phases over a 10-year window.

3. Efficiency assumptions
- Compression and compute-reduction bands based on procedural architecture targets and internal measurements.

4. Annual savings output
- Produce year-by-year savings estimates.

5. Cumulative total
- Sum annual outputs across the 10-year window.

## Why This Is a Scenario Model
The output is intentionally labeled as scenario modeling because real-world impact depends on:
- deployment scale
- region-specific energy mix
- implementation quality
- policy and market adoption timing

## Validation Plan
- Publish and maintain assumptions transparently.
- Compare model outputs against measured implementation telemetry over time.
- Revise scenario parameters as deployment evidence increases.

## Communication Rule
Always present projection with this disclaimer:
"Scenario projection, not guaranteed production baseline."
