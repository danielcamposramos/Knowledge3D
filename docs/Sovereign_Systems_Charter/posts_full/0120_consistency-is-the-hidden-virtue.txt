Consistency Is the Hidden Virtue - by harry blazer harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Consistency Is the Hidden Virtue Lesson From The NFL harry blazer Jan 06, 2026 2 Share One of the least appreciated virtues in modern life is consistency . Not intensity. Not brilliance. Not late-breaking heroics. Consistency. The NFL’s divisional tiebreaker system, oddly enough, offers a rare and lucid example of this principle operating cleanly in the real world. When teams finish with the same record, the league does not ask who looked strongest at the end, who had the most dramatic wins or who felt more deserving. It asks a simpler and more rigorous question: Who performed more consistently against the same opponents, under the same constraints, over time? Division games matter most because they are repeatable, symmetric and unavoidable. You face the same rivals every year. You play them home and away. There is no narrative distortion, no scheduling sleight of hand, no selective memory. You either show up and win or you don’t. The system honors endurance over spikes. This runs counter to modern sensibilities, which overweight recency, momentum and story. We are conditioned to believe that what happens “when it matters most” should matter more. But that framing quietly smuggles in a dangerous idea: that principles are negotiable if the ending is exciting enough. The NFL rejects that idea. Every game counts the same. Every lapse carries weight. There is no redemption arc baked into the rules. The season is treated not as a story, but as a proof. That distinction matters far beyond sports. In ethics, consistency is what separates belief from performance. In markets, it distinguishes sustainable enterprises from speculative flashes. In governance, it is the difference between rule of law and rule by exception. And in questions of human sovereignty, consistency is what anchors responsibility across time, rather than allowing it to be rewritten retroactively by outcomes. Anyone can surge. Anyone can peak. Anyone can look virtuous or competent for a moment. But systems that honor consistency are systems that honor reality — because reality does not grade on excitement. It grades on alignment, repetition and accountability under constraint. That is why the tiebreaker feels harsh to those who don’t understand it — and fair to those who do.
