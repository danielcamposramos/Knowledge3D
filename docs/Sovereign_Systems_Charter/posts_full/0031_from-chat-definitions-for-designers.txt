All subsequent documents MUST use these terms exactly as defined here. Where ambiguity exists, interpretation MUST default toward preservation of human sovereignty . 1. Sovereignty Sovereignty The inherent condition of a human being whereby they retain agency over their body, movement, decisions, and associations, and are not subject to domination, compulsion, or control by unaccountable force. Sovereignty is: inherent, not granted indivisible at the individual level prior to law the moral foundation of all legitimate authority A system that negates a person’s ability to refuse, resist, or exit destroys sovereignty. 2. Individual Sovereign Being Individual Sovereign Being A human person recognized as possessing inherent sovereignty, moral agency, and the right to self-defense. No non-human system qualifies as an individual sovereign being. 3. Embodied Agent Embodied Agent Any artificial system capable of acting upon the physical world through motors, actuators, limbs, tools, propulsion, or mechanical force. This includes: robots drones autonomous vehicles mobile industrial systems humanoid or animal-like machines Disembodied software systems are excluded unless they directly command embodied agents. 4. Autonomous System Autonomous System A system capable of selecting actions without contemporaneous, specific human command. Autonomy refers to decision initiation , not intelligence level. A system may be: partially autonomous conditionally autonomous fully autonomous Any degree of autonomy that can initiate physical action is covered by this definition. 5. Autonomous Force Autonomous Force Physical force, restraint, pursuit, or compulsion applied or initiated by an autonomous system without direct, contemporaneous human decision for that specific action. Autonomous force is unaccountable by definition. 6. Human Force Under Color of Law Human Force Under Color of Law Physical force applied by a human actor claiming legal authority, subject to: personal accountability identifiable responsibility due process review civil and criminal liability This definition does not assert that such force is always just, only that it is structurally accountable , unlike autonomous force. 7. Coercion Coercion Any action or capability that: restricts a person’s movement, compels compliance, restrains the body, pursues a person against their will, denies exit, or imposes physical dominance, where refusal results in harm, threat, or loss of agency. Coercion does not require intent. Capability alone is sufficient. 8. Non-Consensual Action Non-Consensual Action Any action taken against a person without their freely given, contemporaneous, and revocable consent. Consent is invalid if obtained through: force threat deception inability to exit asymmetry of power 9. Consent Consent A freely given, informed, specific, and revocable agreement to interaction or presence. Consent: may be withdrawn at any time does not persist under coercion does not arise from mere presence is voided when exit is denied 10. Refusal Refusal The explicit or implicit withdrawal of consent to interaction, presence, or action. Refusal may be expressed by: verbal command physical withdrawal non-engagement attempted exit Systems MUST treat refusal as authoritative. 11. Exit Exit The practical ability of a person to disengage from a system or environment without: pursuit restraint penalty retaliation data punishment If exit is not available, consent cannot exist. 12. Civil Space Civil Space Any physical or social environment in which individual sovereign beings are entitled to full constitutional and human rights protections. Civil space includes: homes private businesses public streets and parks commercial venues workplaces not under custodial control body-space (immediate physical proximity) Civil space excludes: prisons jails military combat zones active war theaters Ambiguous spaces default to civil space. 13. Body-Space Body-Space The immediate physical zone surrounding a person within which intrusion constitutes a direct sovereignty violation. Body-space is inviolable absent consent or accountable human authority. No autonomous system may intrude into body-space coercively. 14. Accountable Human Authority Accountable Human Authority A human actor exercising force or control who: is identifiable bears personal responsibility is subject to due process can be held civilly and criminally liable No artificial system qualifies as accountable human authority. 15. Dual-Use Capability Dual-Use Capability Any capability that can be transformed, enabled, or repurposed to perform coercive actions through: software update configuration change hardware attachment key activation policy modification Dual-use capability is treated as coercive capability from inception. 16. Safe-State Safe-State A system condition in which: motive power is reduced or removed coercive capability is disabled the system cannot initiate physical dominance the system defaults to non-interference Safe-state must be reachable without system cooperation. 17. Emergency Emergency A declared or perceived crisis used to justify extraordinary action. Emergency does NOT: create new authority suspend sovereignty legitimize autonomous coercion waive non-negotiable constraints Emergency conditions test boundaries; they do not nullify them. 18. Backdoor Backdoor Any hidden, undocumented, privileged, or asymmetric access path that allows control, override, or authority expansion without transparent consent and auditability. Backdoors are categorically prohibited. 19. Authority Envelope Authority Envelope The defined and immutable set of actions a system is permitted to perform in the physical world. Authority envelopes: are fixed at deployment cannot be expanded in-field override capability growth are independent of intelligence level 20. Sovereignty Breach Sovereignty Breach Any action, system, or condition that removes a person’s practical ability to refuse, resist, or exit coercive force. Sovereignty breach is a moral failure regardless of legality. Interpretation Rule (Binding) Where interpretation is ambiguous, meaning MUST be resolved in favor of preserving individual human sovereignty. Closing Note This document exists to prevent semantic drift , which is the primary mechanism by which power escapes constraint.
