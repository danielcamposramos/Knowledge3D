We argue about calories, macros, pyramids, plant-based ideology, the dangers of meat, the virtues of seed oils, the superiority of industrially sterilized foods, and a thousand other doctrines that all share the same flaw: They forget who we are. Human biology is not a mystery. We are not a blank slate. We are not infinitely adaptable. We are not laboratory organisms that can thrive on industrial invention. We are the product of: hundreds of thousands of years of selective pressure millions of years of hominin dietary evolution environments that rewarded nutrient density survival strategies rooted in fat, organ meat, marrow, and seafood And for 99% of our existence, we lived in alignment with this biology. Then, within a few decades — a blink — industrial civilization replaced: nutrient density with nutrient dilution ancestral fats with chemical seed oils real milk with sterilized ultra-pasteurized imitation organ meats with boneless “lean protein” fermented traditional foods with sterile packaged products regenerative soils with synthetic chemicals ecological harmony with agro-industrial extraction What followed was not a mystery. It was inevitable. And in the 1930s, one man saw the collapse forming before the scientific establishment had the conceptual tools to understand it. His name was Weston A. Price. And he identified — visually, repeatedly, irrefutably — the principles that modern biochemistry is now rediscovering. But long before we get to Price, we must first step back into the world he was observing and ask the simplest, most important question in nutrition: How did humans feed themselves before we forgot how to feed ourselves? PART 2 — THE UNIVERSAL PATTERN OF HEALTHY TRADITIONAL PEOPLES Price’s travels took him across continents and climates: Arctic ice fields Swiss alpine valleys remote Pacific Islands African plains Native American nations Gaelic fishing communities Australian aboriginal territories Completely different ecosystems. Completely different staple foods. Completely different cultural traditions. And yet, the pattern was not different at all. Every traditional culture, untouched by industrial foods, shared the same outcomes: broad dental arches straight teeth powerful immunity exceptional skeletal development near-zero cavities robust fertility minimal chronic disease well-formed faces and airways Children were vibrant, resilient, strong, and structurally harmonious. Adults aged gracefully with minimal degeneration. And everywhere Price went, he asked the same question: “What foods do you consider sacred, especially for pregnancy?” The answers were the same in every language. Organ meats Bone marrow Fat Raw dairy Fish eggs Shellfish Liver High-fat broths Egg yolks Sea animals rich in DHA Deep yellow butter from cows on spring grass These foods were set aside for young women, pregnant women, nursing mothers, growing children, and young men preparing for conception. And not as delicacies, but as obligations. The health of the next generation depended on them. PART 3 — WHY ORGAN MEATS AND ANIMAL FATS WERE UNIVERSAL SACRED FOODS Traditional cultures recognized something instinctively that modern science spent decades rediscovering: Organ meats contain the most concentrated, bioavailable nutrients in the natural world. Liver alone contains: retinol (vitamin A) B12 folate choline copper zinc iron CoQ10 peptides that regulate organ growth and immune signaling Bone marrow contains: stem-cell growth factors collagen precursors fat-soluble vitamins unique lipids used for immune regulation Fish eggs contain: DHA and EPA phospholipids for brain development iodine vitamin D B vitamins Raw dairy from grass-fed animals contains: K2 (Price’s Activator X) A and D in synergistic ratio calcium bound to phosphatase immunoglobulins lactoferrin healthy saturated fats CLA butyrate live enzymes and bacteria All the nutrients most essential for: fetal development neurological growth skeletal formation dental arch development immune maturity mitochondrial function endocrine balance This is not ideology — it’s human biochemistry. Plants simply do not contain: retinol DHA EPA MK-4 heme iron bioavailable zinc choline in adequate amounts B12 organ-specific peptides Traditional cultures knew this without laboratories. They knew it through outcomes . They knew it through generational observation . They knew it through the integrity of their children . And everywhere Price traveled, he found the same quiet, sacred truth: Pregnant women were fed organ meats because nothing matches their nutrient density. This is perhaps the clearest nutritional truth humanity has ever discovered. PART 4 — THE BIOCHEMICAL FOUNDATION OF HUMAN NUTRITION We can now explain what healthy cultures knew intuitively. 1. Humans are metabolic omnivores with carnivore-leaning machinery We digest: protein efficiently fat exceptionally well collagen with ease organs with perfect biochemical synergy We do not digest: cellulose (grass) raw starch well anti-nutrient–laden seeds well high-linoleic plant oils without oxidative stress Our stomach acidity matches carnivores. Our small intestine is optimized for nutrient absorbability. Our brain requires DHA, cholesterol, B12, and saturated fat. 2. Fat-soluble vitamins are the master regulators of human development Without A, D, and K2: dental arches narrow teeth crowd bones weaken immune systems collapse fertility declines endocrine systems destabilize fetal development suffers These vitamins exist almost exclusively in animal fats — especially organs. 3. Cholesterol is not a toxin — it is the foundation of life steroid hormones brain structure nerve membranes bile acids immune signaling A low-cholesterol diet is biologically incoherent for humans. 4. Seed oils are a biochemical disaster Industrial seed oils — soybean, canola, sunflower, safflower, cottonseed — did not exist in the human diet until the 20th century. Their introduction correlates tightly with: diabetes fatty liver obesity infertility heart disease neuroinflammation mitochondrial dysfunction Their linoleic acid content is incompatible with human metabolism and accumulates in tissues for 700 days . Traditional cultures used: tallow lard butter ghee duck fat marrow fat These are stable, nutrient-rich, evolutionarily aligned fats. 5. Raw, unprocessed foods contain intact biological information Heat destroys: enzymes immunoglobulins probiotics peptide signaling molecules delicate vitamins Traditional cultures, and Price’s healthiest communities, ate: raw dairy raw cheeses fermented foods lightly cooked meats living, unprocessed sources of nutrition PART 5 — THE GREAT COLLAPSE AND THE MODERN DISEASE MAP When refined flour, sugar, canned milk, industrial oils, and sterilized foods entered traditional villages, Price watched the collapse occur within one generation . He documented: narrowing faces crowded teeth increased cavities weakened immunity higher rates of infection reduced fertility smaller frames inferior skeletal structure increased disease susceptibility This was not genetics. It was nutritional disintegration. We are now living inside the advanced stages of the same pattern. Chronic illness is normal. Degenerative disease is expected. Metabolic dysfunction begins in childhood. Infertility is epidemic. Hormonal disorders are pervasive. Allergies and autoimmune conditions are commonplace. Children require orthodontics for jaws our ancestors never struggled with. We call this “modern life,” but it is simply the biological consequences of violating ancestral nutritional laws . And the path out is the same path Price observed across the world: return to nutrient density return to animal foods return to organ meats return to raw dairy where safe return to ancestral fats return to soil-based agriculture return to ecological nutrition Not because it is trendy. Not because it is nostalgic. Because it is human . This is the nutritional operating system we were built for. PART 6 — THE CAVEAT: THE FOOD MUST BE REAL Everything in this essay assumes the food is authentic. Nutrition cannot be evaluated in isolation from agricultural integrity. For ancestral nutrition to work, the food itself must be: non-GMO from animals not fed GMO feed raised without prophylactic antibiotics not subjected to unnecessary or experimental vaccines free from synthetic pesticides free from herbicides (especially glyphosate) free from irradiation free from additives free from industrial processing raised in regenerative systems grown in living soil produced and handled without contamination aligned with ecological cycles and biological design Most food safety failures — and most nutritional failures — come from violating these principles.
