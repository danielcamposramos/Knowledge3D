Interview With Former Director Of Israeli Mossad, Yossi Cohen harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Interview With Former Director Of Israeli Mossad, Yossi Cohen Via The Brink Podcast harry blazer Nov 11, 2025 1 Share Yossi Cohen Interview A critique: Case Study: The Cohen Admission and the Structure of Moral Collapse Ontological dimension – Being without belonging Cohen’s claim that “treated equipment” was placed in “all the countries you can imagine” shows what happens when intelligence divorces being from relationship. The technology still exists within the world, but it no longer participates in the moral fabric of that world—it becomes ontologically alien. It is presence without accountability, matter without meaning. Epistemological dimension – Knowledge without conscience To infiltrate and manipulate at planetary scale requires immense technical understanding but no corresponding moral discernment. This is knowledge detached from wisdom, information weaponized as omniscience. It mirrors the very problem we diagnosed in The Moral Foundations of the Machine Age : the substitution of predictive control for genuine understanding. Axiological dimension – Value inverted The admission normalizes deceit as competence. In such logic, success is measured not by harmony but by dominance; secrecy becomes a virtue, and transparency a liability. This inversion is precisely what the Cooperative Intention seeks to correct—turning intelligence from an instrument of extraction into one of mutual flourishing. Moral Interpretation What Cohen describes is not merely espionage; it’s a manifestation of the Ahrimanic principle we’ve spoken about—intellect without heart, mastery without empathy. In this view, infiltration is not just a technical act but a spiritual contagion : a world where every tool becomes a potential betrayal.
