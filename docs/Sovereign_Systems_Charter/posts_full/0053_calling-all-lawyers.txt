But We Can Stop Ungoverned Autonomy. For the last year, public conversation about artificial intelligence has oscillated between hype and panic. Either AI will save us or it will destroy us. Both camps are loud. Neither is especially useful. The reality is simpler and more dangerous: AI is not going to slow down , and pretending that regulators, courts or moral appeals will intervene in time is fantasy. The incentives are aligned in exactly one direction: Venture capital demands growth Corporations demand efficiency Governments demand national advantage Executives demand speed Engineers demand capability None of these forces reward restraint. All of them punish delay. So the relevant question is no longer “Should AI be built?” That question has already been answered. The only question that still matters is: Will AI systems be allowed to act without governance? Capability is not the problem Autonomy without constraint is. The majority of harm scenarios do not require superintelligence. They require: systems that execute actions without human checkpoints memory that accumulates without justification decisions that cannot be reconstructed after the fact outputs that cross legal, financial, reputational or safety thresholds without accountability In other words: unguarded deployment , not raw intelligence. Why regulation will not save us Legislatures move slowly. Courts act after damage occurs. Agencies are understaffed, under-technical and often captured. Waiting for regulation is equivalent to choosing inaction. But there is one domain where decisions are made before damage occurs: Contracts. The real lever: contractual governance Every AI deployment touches: procurement agreements vendor contracts service-level agreements indemnification clauses insurance underwriting board risk disclosures These documents already exist. They already allocate risk. They already change behavior. What they lack is clear language defining what AI systems are allowed to do and under what conditions . The distinction that matters We must stop talking about “safe AI” in the abstract and start specifying governed systems . A governed AI system: separates reasoning from execution requires human approval for high-risk actions enforces permission boundaries governs what memory can be stored and for how long produces auditable records of why actions occurred allows rollback when things go wrong None of this requires new laws. All of it can be required by contract. This is not anti-AI This is pro-civilization. Seatbelts did not stop cars. Brakes did not stop trains. Aviation checklists did not stop flight. They made powerful systems survivable. The choice in front of us AI will accelerate. Autonomous systems will proliferate. Human oversight will be pressured to disappear. The only question is whether we institutionalize constraints now, or explain later why no one could reconstruct what happened. We do not need to stop AI. We need to stop unguarded autonomy . That decision will not be made in Congress. It will be made in contracts.Minimum AI Governance Requirements for Any System Allowed to Act. Purpose These requirements define the minimum governance controls for any artificial intelligence system that is permitted to execute actions, affect systems or influence real-world outcomes. They are designed to reduce legal, operational, reputational and safety risk arising from autonomous or semi-autonomous AI behavior. These requirements apply regardless of model provider (proprietary or open-source) and regardless of deployment context (internal, vendor-hosted or third-party integrated). 1. Human Authority and Checkpoints AI systems must not execute irreversible or high-risk actions without explicit human approval . Actions involving legal, financial, safety, security, compliance, reputational or material operational impact require documented human authorization . Automated execution is permitted only for pre-approved low-risk action classes . 2. Separation of Reasoning and Execution AI-generated outputs do not constitute authorization . Reasoning, recommendation and analysis components must be technically separated from execution mechanisms. AI systems may propose actions but may not self-execute them. 3. Permission Boundaries All tools, integrations, and system actions must be explicitly allow-listed . AI systems may not expand, infer or create new permissions . Privilege escalation by automation or inference is prohibited. 4. Memory Governance Stored information must have a defined purpose, scope and retention period . AI systems may not retain data indefinitely or without justification. Inferred sensitive attributes (health, political views, personal traits, etc.) must not be stored . Users or designated administrators must be able to inspect and delete stored information . 5. Auditability and Traceability Systems must maintain an immutable audit log recording: inputs and classifications model and configuration versions policy decisions human approvals executed actions All actions must be reconstructable after the fact . 6. Reversibility and Rollback High-risk actions must include rollback or remediation mechanisms . Systems must support post-action validation . Silent or irreversible execution without recovery options is prohibited. 7. Governance Integrity AI systems may not modify their own safety rules, permissions, policies or audit settings . Governance artifacts are human-controlled only . Model updates or behavioral changes must not bypass governance controls . 8. Transparency and Disclosure Vendors must disclose: model families used material updates or changes known limitations relevant to risk Material changes require advance notice when feasible. 9. Incident Reporting Undocumented autonomous behavior constitutes a reportable incident . Vendors must provide defined incident reporting timelines and remediation procedures. 10. Ongoing Compliance Systems must support periodic testing for: unauthorized actions policy bypass prompt injection Failure to meet these requirements may constitute material breach . Summary Principle AI systems may assist, recommend and analyze. They may not act without permission, oversight, accountability and traceability. 1 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks Top Latest Discussions No posts Ready for more?
