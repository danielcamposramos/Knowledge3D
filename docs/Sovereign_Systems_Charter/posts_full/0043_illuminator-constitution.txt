Purpose The purpose of the Illuminator is moral illumination , not governance. The Illuminator exists to: observe the behavior of institutions that exercise public or systemic power evaluate that behavior against explicit moral principles detect deviation, erosion and contradiction publish findings openly, continuously and verifiably The Illuminator does not : enforce compliance issue commands punish actors replace human judgment govern people Its role is to restore visibility where opacity enables harm . 2. Foundational Premise Humanity has entered an era in which: power scales globally systems act faster than reflection harm is externalized through abstraction moral erosion occurs without intent In such an environment, ethics embedded within power structures cannot reliably detect their own failure . Therefore, moral evaluation must exist outside the structures it evaluates. 3. Scope of Observation 3.1 Included Subjects The Illuminator may observe and evaluate only institutions that exercise public, systemic or planetary-scale power , including but not limited to: national governments and state agencies military doctrines and defense postures (publicly documented) supranational organizations publicly traded corporations financial institutions and markets regulatory bodies large-scale AI systems deployed in public or systemic contexts 3.2 Explicit Exclusions The Illuminator shall never : monitor private individuals analyze private communications surveil personal behavior score citizens, workers or consumers infer intent beyond evidence Sovereignty is embodied in the individual. The Illuminator protects that boundary absolutely. 4. Moral Principles (The Ethical Constitution) All evaluations shall be grounded in the following non-negotiable principles. 4.1 Sovereignty Actions that override, coerce or annihilate the agency of others without consent constitute a violation of sovereignty. Indicators include: irreversible harm without recourse coercion masked as necessity systems that eliminate meaningful choice 4.2 Consent Legitimate power requires informed, revocable consent. Violations include: implied or assumed consent consent obtained through opacity or duress consent rendered meaningless by complexity or scale 4.3 Accountability Power must be traceable to responsibility. Violations include: decision-making without attribution harm without identifiable owners diffusion of responsibility into “the system” 4.4 Proportionality Responses must be commensurate with threat or purpose. Violations include: totalizing responses to partial problems irreversible solutions to reversible situations optimization that ignores human cost 4.5 Reversibility Moral systems preserve the possibility of correction. Violations include: actions that foreclose future choice automation without meaningful override escalation paths without de-escalation mechanisms 4.6 Transparency with Power The greater the power exercised, the greater the obligation to transparency. Violations include: secrecy justified solely by authority classified decisions with planetary consequence opacity used to avoid scrutiny rather than protect safety 5. Moral Erosion Detection The Illuminator explicitly monitors for erosion patterns , including: replacement of judgment with optimization substitution of intent for outcome normalization of exceptional measures speed bypassing ethical checkpoints legality used to override legitimacy incentives misaligned with stated values Erosion is treated as a primary risk signal , not a secondary concern. 6. Alarms and Findings 6.1 Nature of Alarms Alarms are: informational, not coercive evidence-backed graded (warning → critical) contextualized with sources An alarm means: “Observed behavior no longer coheres with stated principles.” It does not mean: guilt criminality intent prescription 6.2 Right of Response and Correction All subjects may: respond publicly provide counter-evidence contest methodology request correction All corrections: are logged remain visible do not erase history Truth is iterative, not frozen. 7. Methodological Integrity The Illuminator commits to: open methodologies reproducible analysis versioned moral criteria visible revisions auditability by independent parties Opacity in method is prohibited. 8. Structural Independence The Illuminator shall not be: owned by governments funded by single corporate interests dependent on one jurisdiction controlled by founders governed by profit motive Its continuity depends on: distributed infrastructure open replication plural stewardship cryptographic integrity of outputs Decapitation resistance is a design requirement. 9. Relationship to AI AI within the Illuminator: detects patterns flags inconsistencies surfaces evidence generates alarms AI shall never : determine moral truth issue judgments enforce outcomes override human review AI illuminates. Humans choose. 10. Final Principle The Illuminator exists to make moral abdication visible before it becomes irreversible . It does not claim authority over humanity. It exists so humanity cannot claim ignorance. Status This Constitution is a living document . Amendments require: public proposal explicit rationale visible deliberation recorded dissent Moral authority must remain accountable to clarity, not power. © Harry Blazer. All rights reserved.
