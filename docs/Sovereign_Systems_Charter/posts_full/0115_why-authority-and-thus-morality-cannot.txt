Why Authority (and thus morality) Cannot Be Outsourced harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Why Authority (and thus morality) Cannot Be Outsourced “You can get advice from many places. But in the end, you are responsible for the choices you make. You cannot surrender your accountability. You cannot outsource the consequences of your choices.” harry blazer Jan 08, 2026 1 Share There is a distinction that modern civilization repeatedly fails to make, and the cost of that failure is visible everywhere. It is the difference between guidance and authority . You can receive guidance from many places. You cannot surrender responsibility for the choices you make. This distinction is not theological. It is not ideological. It is not even philosophical in the abstract. It is a statement about how reality actually behaves. Intelligence is not the problem One of the great errors of modern debate is the assumption that acknowledging intelligence beyond the human somehow threatens human dignity or agency. It does not. Intelligence of various kinds clearly exceeds the individual human mind: collective intelligence emergent intelligence institutional intelligence biological intelligence technological intelligence possibly even non-human or non-incarnate intelligence Recognizing this is not weakness. It is honesty. The problem has never been intelligence beyond the human. The problem is authority beyond the human . The non-delegation of responsibility You may consult: teachers texts traditions systems models gods spirits machines You may be informed by them. You may be corrected by them. You may even be transformed by them. But choice remains local . No matter how elevated the source of guidance, the act of choosing still occurs in you. No matter how persuasive the authority, the consequences still unfold through your actions. Responsibility does not transfer. It cannot be signed away. It cannot be deferred. It cannot be laundered through obedience. This is not a moral opinion. It is a structural feature of causality. Where religions quietly fail Many religions collapse precisely at this point. The instruction is familiar: Give yourself to God. Submit to divine will. Obey, and you will be saved. The promise is comforting. The mechanism is corrosive. What actually happens is subtle: authority is externalized obedience is moralized accountability is blurred consequences remain fully human The believer acts. The believer chooses. The believer causes harm or good. But when reckoning comes, the justification is ready-made: “I was following God’s will.” Reality does not recognize that defense. History makes this plain. Atrocities do not require evil intent when authority has been outsourced. They only require obedience without ownership. This is not a rejection of God. It is a rejection of abdication . Jesus did not remove responsibility This is where institutional Christianity diverges sharply from its own source. Jesus does not erase human agency. He intensifies it. He repeatedly turns moral responsibility back onto the individual. The Kingdom of God is within you is not mystical reassurance. It is the internalization of authority. The later transformation of this message into a system of submission is not continuity. It is inversion. The modern replay: AI and outsourced judgment What religion once did through God, modernity now does through systems. Replace: “God says” with “The model recommends” The structure is identical. The human becomes an operator, not an author. Decision-making is framed as optimization. Responsibility is diffused across systems, committees, models, policies. And when harm occurs, the defense is already prepared: “I followed the system.” Again, reality does not recognize that defense. Alignment is not authorship. Free will, determinism and the wrong question The endless debate over free will versus determinism persists because it avoids the one question that actually matters: Who bears the consequences? Even in a fully deterministic universe: actions still occur effects still propagate harm and benefit still manifest responsibility still attaches Determinism may explain why something happened. It does not absorb the moral cost of it happening. Responsibility is invariant under explanation. That is why it cannot be outsourced. The sovereignty axiom This can be stated formally, without theology or metaphysics: No agent may outsource moral accountability for choices whose consequences it participates in causing. This applies to: individuals religions institutions governments corporations artificial intelligence systems It is substrate-independent. And because it is, it survives every attempt to evade it. The quiet truth You may be guided by many intelligences. You will only ever be judged by the consequences of your own choices. That is not a threat. It is not a punishment. It is the condition that makes meaning possible. Without it, morality collapses into compliance, and intelligence becomes dangerous precisely because it is powerful without responsibility. Sovereignty is not the absence of guidance. It is the refusal to abdicate authorship. And that refusal may be the most human act left to us. 1 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks Top Latest Discussions No posts Ready for more?
