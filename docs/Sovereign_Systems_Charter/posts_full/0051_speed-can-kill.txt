This is not a metaphor. It is physics. In racing, the relationship is brutally clear. Speed itself is not the enemy. The enemy is speed that exceeds the driver’s ability to control the car or the car’s ability to produce the intended outcome under real conditions. When that envelope is violated, the consequences are not subtle. The car leaves the track. People get hurt. Sometimes killed. There is no debate phase. No post-hoc justification. No amount of intention changes the result. The same law applies everywhere humans remain accountable: aviation, medicine, finance, nuclear energy — and now, artificial intelligence. The Control Envelope Every responsible system operates within what engineers call an envelope : the boundary within which safe, predictable control is possible. That envelope is shaped by: human judgment and reaction time environmental conditions mechanical or computational limits visibility into system behavior the ability to intervene or stop When any system accelerates beyond that envelope, failure is not a risk — it is a certainty. The only unknown is when . AI and the Disabled Brakes Problem Modern AI culture treats speed as virtue. Faster deployment. Faster iteration. Faster decision-making. Faster automation. What is rarely discussed is what has been quietly removed to enable that speed: human checkpoints meaningful oversight interpretability the ability to say “stop” responsibility that can be clearly located In racing terms, we have built cars that accelerate faster than any driver can manage, placed them on unfamiliar tracks, removed the brakes and called every crash an “edge case.” This is not innovation. It is negligence with better marketing. Why “Non-Attachment to Outcomes” Is Often Misunderstood Non-attachment is frequently misread as indifference. It is not. A skilled driver is not attached to winning in a way that overrides survival. They are deeply attached to staying within control — moment by moment — adjusting speed to conditions, limits, and reality as it unfolds. Non-attachment means: I will not chase an outcome beyond my ability to remain responsible for it. That principle is older than technology. It is embedded in every safety discipline humans have ever developed. AI is the first domain where we are attempting to pretend it no longer applies. Living Thinking and the Refusal to Crash Faster Living Thinking begins with a refusal: We refuse to let intelligence outrun accountability. This is not an argument against AI. It is an argument against removing the driver. AI systems are extraordinarily powerful vehicles. They amplify cognition the way engines amplify force. But amplification without stewardship does not produce progress — it produces wreckage at scale. We should all insist on a simple, unfashionable constraint: A system must never operate faster than the human capacity to understand, intervene, and remain accountable for its consequences. This means: slowing deployment when comprehension lags choosing restraint over advantage accepting delayed outcomes over irreversible harm privileging control over speed In a culture addicted to acceleration, this looks like weakness. In reality, it is the only reason the driver and car survive. The Inevitability of the Crash History is unambiguous. Every system that prioritized speed over control eventually collapsed: financial markets optimized beyond comprehension industrial systems scaled beyond safety margins technologies deployed faster than their consequences could be understood AI will not be the exception. It will be the largest instance. The question is not whether crashes will occur. The question is whether humans will still be in a position to learn from them — or whether responsibility will already have been automated away. The Real Choice The future is not a race between humans and machines. It is a race between: systems that preserve human judgment under scale and systems that disable it in pursuit of speed Living Thinking chooses the first path. Not because it is safer in the short term. But because it is the only path that allows responsibility, repair, and meaning to survive at all. Speed without control is not progress. It is deferred catastrophe. And physics, unlike ideology, always collects its debt. May I suggest the following: The Control Envelope Principle Any system that operates faster or more powerfully than the human capacity to understand, intervene, and remain accountable for its consequences will inevitably fail. 1 3 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks harry blazer Feb 5 Edited Author Thanks much.
