AND NOW THE DETAILS (The Official Start of Part 1) The Core Mechanisms of the Great Taking Think of the system as five stacked layers: Legal code (UCC, securities law, bankruptcy law) Custody & settlement infrastructure (DTCC, Cede & Co, Euroclear, Clearstream) Intermediaries (brokers, banks, custodians) Central bank + primary dealers (Fed, ECB, BoE, etc.) Crisis / resolution regimes (bail-in laws, resolution authority, emergency powers) Each layer quietly shifts risk downwards and legal priority upwards . Article 8 / “Security Entitlements”: Abolition of Direct Ownership Key idea: You don’t own the asset. You own a “security entitlement” — a bundle of rights against an intermediary who is the real legal owner. Under modern UCC Article 8 (in the US) and equivalent in other jurisdictions: The intermediary (broker, custodian) typically holds securities with a central depository (like DTCC’s Cede & Co) in fungible bulk , not as individually earmarked assets Your claim is to an “interest” in that pool, not to a specific share certificate with your name on it If the intermediary fails, you stand in line with other entitlement holders, subject to the bankruptcy / resolution rules and the priority of secured creditors So: Before dematerialization: you had a certificate with your name After dematerialization: you have an account statement and a claim The whole system runs on IOUs about IOUs . This is exactly the sort of “engineered structure” we’ve been identifying in other domains: It improves efficiency and liquidity But it simultaneously degrades individual sovereignty over property You moved from: “I own this” → “Someone powerful promises that I own this.” And the law is crystal clear which one actually matters. Centralized Custody: Cede & Co as the “One Ring” At the heart of the US equity market: DTCC (Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation) is the main clearing/settlement infrastructure Its nominee, Cede & Co , is recorded as the legal owner of most publicly traded securities Brokers and custodians hold beneficial interests on their books You hold an entitlement at the very end of the chain This means: There is effectively one legal owner of most US equities: Cede & Co Everyone else is in a cascading chain of claims In a benign system, this is “just plumbing.” In a predatory system, this is the perfect confiscation scaffold : You don’t need to seize millions of individual accounts You just need to control the apex of the custody pyramid and the legal rules governing entitlements in a crisis Rehypothecation and Leverage: Pledging What Isn’t Really Yours Because intermediaries are legally the holders of record, they can: Pledge client assets as collateral Engage in rehypothecation (pledging the same collateral multiple times in chains of deals) Use your entitlements to support their leverage So the same asset can be: Economically “owned” by you Legally controlled by your broker Pledged to a prime broker Pledged again to another institution All sitting inside a clearing / custody system ultimately controlled by a handful of entities When everyone is leveraged into everyone else , a systemic event doesn’t just cause losses — it triggers a scramble for collateral . And who gets satisfied first in that scramble? Secured creditors and systemically important institutions Not retail holders Not pensioners Not municipalities Again: structure reveals intent . Bail-Ins and Resolution Regimes: Legalized Conversion of Your Claims Post-2008, many jurisdictions (US, EU, UK, others) introduced: Bail-in regimes : instead of using taxpayer money to bail out failing banks, you can haircut or convert certain creditor claims (including large depositors, bond holders) into equity to recapitalize the institution Resolution authorities : agencies empowered to step in early, impose stays on withdrawals, and restructure the balance sheet “for financial stability” On paper, this is sold as: “We’re protecting taxpayers and the system.” In reality, it encodes: In a crisis, your “asset” is actually the system’s recapitalization buffer. Your deposits, your securities entitlements, your “cash equivalents” — all become raw material for stabilizing the institutions that failed. Put simply: Your perception = I hold an asset at a bank or broker The system’s perspective = the bank or broker holds a liability to you that can be modified, frozen, bailed in or subordinated in a crisis The structure is not neutral. It pre-decides that you will be sacrificed to preserve the top of the pyramid.
