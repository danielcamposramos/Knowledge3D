THE MODERN DAY CHRISTMAS IS NOT A CELEBRATION OF JESUS harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in THE MODERN DAY CHRISTMAS IS NOT A CELEBRATION OF JESUS Christmas Is A Spectacle - A Sedative Disguised As A Palliative harry blazer Dec 11, 2025 1 Share There is no world in which Jesus would be comfortable with the way his birth is celebrated today. Christmas, as it stands, is not a tribute to his life but an inversion of it. And the more honestly we look, the clearer it becomes that the holiday has been repurposed from something sacred into something strategic — a seasonal mind-control operation that keeps people compliant. On the surface, Christmas looks like devotion. Songs, lights, praise, ritual, nostalgia. But beneath the glow lies the contradiction: we celebrate the birth of a man who confronted empire while we live inside systems that perpetuate injustice and corruption without pause. Jesus demanded transformation, not pageantry. He overturned tables. He lifted the oppressed. He spoke against power. Yet Christmas teaches the opposite. It encourages people to feel spiritual without becoming accountable. It offers sentiment in place of sovereignty. It substitutes spectable for responsibility. Something pure has been inverted into a mechanism of pacification. A call to conscience has morphed into sedation. A message of liberation has been engineered into a distraction. A revolutionary birth story now serves to stabilize the very forces Jesus challenged. If Jesus walked into a modern Christmas celebration, he would not see his message living. He would see it inverted. He would see the sacred being used to pacify rather than awaken. He would see people numbed by tradition instead of driven to moral courage. And he would ask the same question he asked two thousand years ago: How did something sacred become a tool for the very forces it was meant to oppose? Reclaiming Christmas is not about rejecting joy. It is about refusing inversion. It is about sovereignty — the courage to see clearly and act accordingly. It is about restoring the sacred to what is actually sacred: that love and justice be lived, not extravagandized. 1 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks Top Latest Discussions No posts Ready for more?
