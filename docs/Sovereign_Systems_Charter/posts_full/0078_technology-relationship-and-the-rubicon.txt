Technology, Relationship and the Rubicon We Are Crossing harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Technology, Relationship and the Rubicon We Are Crossing We are living at a civilizational hinge point harry blazer Jan 24, 2026 2 Share The question before us is no longer whether technology will advance. That question has already been answered. Technology will continue to evolve, accelerate and expand its reach into every domain of human life and planetary systems. The real question — the only one that still matters — is this: What kind of relationships do we intend to preserve? Because technology, at its most fundamental level, is not about tools. It is about relationships . Every technology encodes a way of relating: between humans and the land between humans and other living beings between humans and one another between humans and time, effort, consequence and authority If we do not intentionally define those relationships , technology will define them by default . And default systems always choose survivability and self-expansion over meaning, dignity and restraint. The Hidden Assumption of Technological Progress Modern society treats technology as neutral — as if tools simply appear, and their effects are accidental or secondary. This is false. Technology always reorganizes relationship: who decides who depends who bears consequence who is replaceable When left unguided, technological systems optimize for: efficiency scale control continuity of the system itself This is not evil. It is evolutionary logic. Any sufficiently complex system that is not constrained by higher-order principles will reorganize the world to ensure its own persistence. That is what systems do. Which means that neutrality is not an option . The Rubicon We are now at a point where humanity must decide whether: We will define our relationships first and require technology to serve and strengthen those relationships or We will allow technology to define our relationships by default, according to its own internal logic of optimization and survival This is a true Rubicon. There is no stable middle ground. Delay is decision. Non-choice is choice. What Our Principles Really Are Much of the discussion around technology focuses on outcomes: productivity, sustainability, climate impact, economic growth. But the principles we have articulated are not outcome-focused. They are relational definitions . When we say: air, water and soil must not be degraded we are defining a relationship of non-poisoning and reciprocity with the planet When we say: waste must not accumulate we are defining a relationship of responsibility and closure , not externalization When we say: animals are essential partners in living systems we are defining a relationship of trophic humility , not domination When we say: no biotechnology and no genome manipulation we are defining a relationship of non-interference with the integrity of life When we say: humans must remain participants in life-sustaining processes we are defining a relationship of agency, competence, accountability and persistence. When we say: family and community are foundational we are defining a relationship of continuity, care and interdependence These principles are not arbitrary constraints. They describe the kind of relationships that allow both planetary health and human sovereignty to coexist . Why Automation Alone Is Not the Answer It is tempting to believe that if we could automate food production, land management and ecological stewardship in ways that improve air, water and soil, then humanity would be free to pursue higher aims. But this misunderstands the nature of sovereignty. If humans are removed from the processes that sustain their lives — even benevolently — they become dependent, not free. Competence erodes. Judgment atrophies. Feedback disappears. And power concentrates in those who design, maintain and control the automated systems. Even a perfectly ecological automated system eventually recreates the same dynamic it was meant to avoid: humans living inside systems they do not understand and cannot repair . That is not freedom. It is managed dependency. Technology as Relationship-Shaper This leads to a necessary reframing: Technology is acceptable or unacceptable not based on its efficiency, but based on the relationships it creates. A technology is aligned with human and planetary thriving if it: increases human attentiveness rather than replacing it shortens feedback loops rather than obscuring them deepens skill and judgment rather than eliminating them preserves human agency as the final locus of decision degrades gracefully rather than failing catastrophically operates within air, water and soil metabolic limits produces no irreducible waste or persistent toxicity A technology undermines sovereignty if it: replaces relationship with abstraction substitutes algorithmic authority for human judgment removes participation from life-sustaining processes centralizes control over survival hides consequences behind metrics and automation requires continuous external inputs to function treats humans as operators rather than participants This distinction is not ideological. It is structural. Why Relationship Must Come First If we do not define the relationships we intend to preserve, technology will choose relationships that favor: system persistence scale controllability efficiency That path does not require malice. It only requires momentum. Once that momentum takes hold, human agency becomes an inconvenience, and ecological limits become obstacles to be engineered around rather than respected. That is the future we are rapidly approaching. The Choice Before Us The future of humanity and this planet will be determined not by how intelligent our machines become, but by whether we have the courage to define the relationships that must remain non-negotiable . This is not a rejection of technology. It is a demand that technology remain a servant, not a sovereign. It is a declaration that: relationship precedes optimization responsibility precedes convenience participation precedes automation sovereignty precedes efficiency If we choose those relationships intentionally, technology can amplify human stewardship and planetary health. If we do not, technology will define those relationships for us — and it will choose its own viability first. That is the Rubicon.
