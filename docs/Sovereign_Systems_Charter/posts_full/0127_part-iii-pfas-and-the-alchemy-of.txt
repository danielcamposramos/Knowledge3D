PART III PFAS and the Alchemy of Permanence harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in PART III PFAS and the Alchemy of Permanence How civilization became the test environment — and accountability decayed faster than contamination harry blazer Jan 04, 2026 3 Share If nuclear power taught the state how to socialize catastrophic risk and software taught society how to tolerate dysfunction, IT and PFAS taught industry something even more radical: Real-world deployment can replace testing, and the public can be made to pay for debugging. This was not an accident. It was the logical next step. From Debugging Code to Debugging Reality By the time the IT industry normalized the idea that: products ship unfinished users encounter failures fixes arrive later (if at all) it had quietly inverted a core assumption of civilization: Prototyping used to precede deployment. Now deployment is the prototype. The costs — time, inconvenience, damage, adaptation — were externalized to the user. PFAS applied this same logic to chemistry. Only this time, the “users” were entire populations. PFAS: Chemistry Designed to Never Fail PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) were engineered to be: Thermally stable Chemically inert Hydrophobic and oleophobic Resistant to degradation From an engineering standpoint, they were a triumph. From a biological standpoint, they were a catastrophe waiting to be discovered. They were perfect for: Military firefighting foams Aerospace applications Electronics Industrial coatings Food packaging Textiles In short: strategic and essential uses . DuPont and the Field Test That Never Ended DuPont was a central actor in the development and production of PFAS compounds, including PFOA (C8) used in Teflon manufacturing. Internal records later showed: Early awareness of persistence Early evidence of bioaccumulation Early signals of health risk But here is the crucial point: There was no viable laboratory model for long-term, population-wide chemical persistence. So the system did what it had already learned to do in software: It shipped anyway. Civilization as the Beta Environment PFAS were not tested for permanence. They were tested by permanence. Water systems became test rigs Human blood became assay medium Wildlife became longitudinal data Time itself became the experiment This is customer-subsidized debugging , scaled to society. Just as software companies learned that users would tolerate crashes if the tool was indispensable, chemical manufacturers learned that: harm delayed is harm discounted causation dispersed is causation denied persistence outlives outrage The Nuclear Parallel Is Exact PFAS are often called “forever chemicals.” That phrasing is not metaphorical. Structurally, PFAS function like chemical radiation : Nuclear wastePFASInvisibleInvisiblePersistentPersistentBioaccumulativeBioaccumulativeLong latencyLong latencyCleanup externalizedCleanup externalizedResponsibility dilutedResponsibility diluted The difference is visibility. Nuclear contamination is localized. PFAS contamination is ambient. That makes PFAS easier to live with and harder to fight . Liability Engineering: When Debugging Becomes Litigation Once harm becomes undeniable, the legal system does not reverse the experiment. It manages its aftermath . The pattern is now familiar: Spin off liability-bearing entities Stretch settlements over decades Avoid admissions of fault Preserve core industrial knowledge Normalize cleanup as public obligation This is why structures like Chemours matter (A Corporate Liability Shield). Chemours did not invent PFAS. It inherited the debugging phase . Why This Was Inevitable After IT Software taught industry that: failures can be patched later users will adapt inconvenience is survivable accountability is optional PFAS applied that lesson to matter itself. But chemistry does not patch. It persists. And when debugging happens at the level of biology, the fix window is measured in generations . The Moral Inversion Completed In classical industry: testing protected the public failure was internalized reputation enforced restraint In the modern immunity regime: deployment replaces testing the public absorbs failure law and time dissolve responsibility This is not negligence. It is a new operating assumption . Software normalized the idea that users should tolerate unfinished systems.
