Preamble – The Source of All Authority All just authority begins in the conscience of the individual. Every human being, by birthright of reason and moral awareness, holds the original claim to sovereignty. Government is therefore not a creator of rights but a trustee of them —a tool formed by free people to coordinate their mutual defense and flourishing. Whenever that instrument forgets its origin, power inverts: the servant becomes the master, and the citizen the subject. The twentieth century perfected that inversion. In 1913, two acts—the Sixteenth Amendment and the Federal Reserve Act—re-wired the architecture of sovereignty itself. The first made labor taxable; the second turned money into debt. Together they shifted the republic from a community of producers to an economy of obligation. The citizen became collateral. What follows is a reclamation: a statement of first principles for a civilization that remembers who truly governs. II. The Principles of Derived Sovereignty Article 1 – Origin of Power All political authority is delegated from the people. It has no independent existence. Article 2 – Limits of Delegation No government may claim or exercise a right that free individuals could not morally exercise themselves. Article 3 – Consent and Voluntariness Consent is not a one-time signature but a living covenant—revocable, reviewable, and renewed through understanding. Article 4 – Transparency and Accountability Authority exists only in the light. Secrecy is the shadow where legitimacy dies. Article 5 – Justice and Reciprocity Lawful power must protect liberty, not privilege; equality before the law precedes efficiency under it. Article 6 – Representation, Not Rule Officials are fiduciaries, not rulers. Their oath binds them to serve, not to command. Article 7 – Revocation of Trust When government ceases to act in good faith, sovereignty reverts to its source: the people themselves. III. Economic Sovereignty – The Moral Architecture of Value The health of a republic rests upon who defines value and who issues it. When money is created as debt and sustained by compulsory taxation, citizens no longer co-create their economy—they underwrite it. The results: Transparency: every allocation is traceable and public. Responsibility: citizens fund what they truly value. Accountability: institutions must earn trust to earn revenue. Guardrails Against Tyranny – The Limits of Majority Rule Majority vote cannot sanctify injustice. Fifty-one percent of a populace has no right to do what one percent may not.
