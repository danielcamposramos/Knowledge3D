## Summary
When moral narratives are used to suppress accounting, systems fail silently — until they fail loudly. Why This Matters for Energy Policy Rebound effects explain why many energy transitions disappoint even when technologies “work as designed.” They also explain why: Efficiency gains fail to reduce total emissions Energy demand continues to rise despite technical improvements Complexity increases rather than stabilizes Margins shrink while dependencies multiply Most importantly, rebound effects expose the category error at the heart of modern energy thinking: We treat efficiency as a substitute for limits. In practice, this means natural gas, nuclear, or hydro must remain fully available, synchronized, staffed, fueled and ready — even if policy claims they are being “phased out.” Wind does not replace dispatchable power.
