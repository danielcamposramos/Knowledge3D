The spot price — the number you see on screens, derived from futures and unallocated contracts — is diverging sharply from the price of real, physical metal. Coins and bars are selling at premiums that can no longer be dismissed as temporary anomalies or retail bottlenecks. This is not a normal pattern. It is the early stage of a market stress cycle—one that I have seen before. In 1979–1980, during the famous silver squeeze, I watched the exchange structure buckle under the pressure of physical demand. And I watched how the institutions responded — not to protect investors, but to protect themselves. Today’s conditions are different in many ways, but the underlying dynamics rhyme. If anything, the forces now at work are deeper, broader and more global. This essay is part analysis, part warning and part roadmap for how to interpret the weeks and months ahead. And unlike most commentators, this perspective comes from experience. I was there the last time the system broke. Paper Is Not Physical — And the Spread Is Widening The gold and silver “spot price” comes from futures markets like COMEX and from unallocated, fractional-reserve pools in London and beyond. These are paper claims on metal, not the metal itself. In quiet times, paper and physical track each other closely enough that most people assume they are the same thing. But when stress enters the system, they diverge — sometimes violently. That’s happening now. Three independent physical markets are tightening simultaneously: Retail coin markets (Maple Leafs, Eagles, Krugerrands, etc.) Retail/wholesale bar markets (1 oz, 10 oz, 1 kg, and even 100 oz products) Exchange-deliverable bar markets (COMEX 100 oz bars, LBMA 400 oz bars) This is crucial to understand: retail shortages do not automatically signal wholesale shortages — but when both begin tightening at the same time, the futures markets come under pressure. And when futures markets come under pressure, the pricing mechanism itself is at risk. What It Looked Like Last Time In 1980, the price of silver soared toward $50 per ounce. The Hunts were accumulating both physical bars and futures contracts. The metal was flowing out of warehouses. Confidence in paper claims was weakening. What happened next is the part few remember — but it is the part that matters. The COMEX exchange simply changed the rules. They declared “liquidation only.” You could sell, but not buy. No new long positions were allowed. Margins were raised repeatedly. Liquidity evaporated. Silver went limit-down day after day. I recognized immediately what had happened — the exchange had essentially declared war on the longs. I exited the day after the rule change. Many others didn’t. And once the market went limit-down, they couldn’t. That is what happens in a system where paper claims exceed physical reality: when the strain becomes too great, the rules are rewritten. Not to preserve fairness. Not to protect investors. But to preserve the system. Why Today Is Different — and More Dangerous Unlike 1980, today’s strain is global : Central banks are accumulating record amounts of physical gold. Asia is absorbing gold and silver at premiums far above Western spot. Industrial silver demand is exploding. COMEX registered inventories are thinning. LBMA vault stocks are in long-term decline. More contract holders are standing for delivery instead of rolling. Retail bar and coin shortages are emerging simultaneously. There is no Hunt brothers to blame this time. This is not a corner attempt. This is structural demand. And while exchanges can change rules, raise margins or force cash settlement, they cannot change reality: If enough people want physical, the paper market loses control. That is the core tension today. What a Modern Breakdown Might Look Like A modern precious metals breakdown will not look like 1980. It will look like this: 1. Physical premiums widen further This has already begun. 2. COMEX delivery demand rises Also happening. 3. Futures curve slips into backwardation A sign of near-term physical scarcity. 4. Rule changes arrive Margins raised, position limits imposed, “liquidation only” whispers return. 5. Load-outs from COMEX vaults accelerate This is the modern form of a bank run. 6. Paper markets disconnect from physical Two prices emerge: the futures price the actual price you must pay for metal And then the world will discover what few understand: The real price of gold is not printed on a screen. The real price of silver is not the COMEX close. The real price of metal is the price at which real metal changes hands. Everything else is a claim. How to Think About This Moment This is not financial advice. It is a statement of structural reality: Paper markets can be halted. Paper markets can be cash-settled. Paper markets can be frozen. Physical metal cannot. Exchanges exist to protect themselves and their largest members. When stress becomes existential, they will — as they did in 1980 — change the rules. But physical holders are not subject to those rules. That is the difference. Why This Matters Now We are in a moment where: gold is being remonetized by emerging nations silver is being devoured by industry physical demand is rising at every level paper markets are thin relative to global claims trust in institutions is eroding and the divergence between futures and physical is widening Whether this resolves smoothly or breaks abruptly will depend on how long exchanges can maintain confidence in paper claims. But the pressures are real. The patterns are familiar. And the history is clear. I lived through the last major breakdown. I recognize the signals. This time, the forces are larger. The stakes are higher. And the consequences — for currencies, for trust, for the financial system itself — may be much more profound. We are entering a period where physical reality is beginning to override financial abstraction. When that happens, price is not discovered in the futures pits. It is discovered in the vault. And in the hands of those who actually hold the metal. How the Gold & Silver Markets Are Really Controlled And Why the Big Players Always Win Harry Blazer & GPT-5, 2025 Most people think gold and silver trade on “free markets.” They don’t. Price is not set by supply and demand for physical metal. It is set by a paper system designed by large institutions, enforced by exchanges, and exploited to accumulate real metal at the expense of everyone else. Here is the distilled version of how it works — the part no mainstream outlet explains. 1. Paper Controls the Price — Not Metal The spot price you see online is based on: COMEX futures LBMA unallocated contracts OTC derivatives ETFs like GLD and SLV These are not ounces of metal. They are claims on the idea of metal — created at ratios of 50:1, 100:1, sometimes higher. This allows big players to sell huge amounts of “gold” or “silver” without owning any metal at all — pushing prices down on screens while physical demand remains unchanged. Paper creates the illusion of abundance. 2. The Physical Market Is Actually Three Separate Markets The physical world is divided into: Retail coins (Maple Leafs, Eagles, Krugerrands) Retail & wholesale bars (1 oz to 1 kg) Exchange-deliverable bars (100 oz / 400 oz) These markets have different inventories, buyers, bottlenecks, and rules. Retail coin shortages don’t always mean wholesale shortages… and wholesale shortages don’t always show up in futures — until they do. When all three tighten at once, the paper market becomes unstable. That’s beginning to happen now. 3. How Big Players Push Prices Down to Accumulate Metal When institutions want to accumulate physical metal cheaply, they: Dump large paper short positions during thin liquidity hours Trigger stop-loss orders, margin calls, and algorithmic selling Force spot price down sharply Buy real metal from panicked wholesalers and refiners who need liquidity Price collapse → physical accumulation → later revaluation. This cycle only works because paper markets dominate price discovery. 4. How Physical Scarcity Pushes Prices Up — But Only After Accumulation Once the big players finish buying: They close their paper shorts They increase delivery demands Inventories begin to drain Premiums on bars and coins rise Asia pays more than Western spot COMEX/LBMA stocks fall Backwardation sometimes appears Only then do prices rise — after the big players are fully positioned. Retail buys late. Retail gets hurt. This happens every cycle. 5. How Exchanges Protect the System — Not You When physical demand threatens the paper market, exchanges intervene. They can: raise margin requirements impose trading limits halt trading suspend new long positions restrict physical withdrawals force cash settlement declare “liquidation only” This isn’t hypothetical. This is exactly what happened in 1980 during the silver squeeze. Silver went limit-down for days. Longs were trapped. Many were wiped out. The Hunts were destroyed. Retail investors were devastated. The system was preserved. The insiders were protected. This is how it works. 6. The Winners and Losers Are Always the Same Winners: central banks bullion banks sovereign buyers hedge funds with exchange access institutions positioned physically before the move Losers: retail futures traders small investors anyone holding paper when the rules change anyone who buys after the surge begins Manipulation, accumulation, revaluation. The cycle is always the same. 7. Why This Matters Right Now Today, for the first time in decades, all the stress indicators are rising at once: retail coin premiums widening retail bar shortages wholesale bar tightness COMEX registered inventory thinning LBMA vault stocks declining Asia absorbing physical at record rates central banks buying more gold than at any time in modern history futures contracts increasingly being “stood for delivery” The physical world is waking up, and the paper world is trying to keep control. This is exactly the environment where the pricing mechanism becomes vulnerable — and where big institutions prepare for the next accumulation cycle. 8. The Only Real Protection There is only one way to step outside this structure: Hold physical metal. Not ETFs. Not futures. Not unallocated accounts. Not paper claims. Physical metal is the only form of gold or silver that cannot be: halted cashed out paper-settled fractionally reserved suspended re-hypothecated rule-changed It is the only form of sovereignty that does not depend on trust in a system that has repeatedly shown it does not deserve it. Closing Thought This isn’t conspiracy. It is structural design. Paper is used to suppress. Physical is used to accumulate. Rules are rewritten when necessary. And the metal always flows upward (to the big players in the know and in control). Understanding the game is the first step in not being played by it.
