Living in a Frequency Soup - by harry blazer harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Living in a Frequency Soup PART 1: Biology in an Engineered Electromagnetic World harry blazer Feb 01, 2026 2 Share For the entirety of evolutionary history, life on Earth developed within a specific electromagnetic environment. That environment was not silent, but it was stochastic — dominated by irregular, low-intensity, naturally occurring electromagnetic fields generated by lightning, solar activity, the Earth’s magnetic field and cosmic background radiation. There were rhythms and resonances, but they were weakly coupled, transient and embedded in noise. In just over a century, we replaced that environment. We now live inside a dense, continuous, information-bearing electromagnetic lattice of our own making — a global frequency soup composed of power grids, radio transmitters, cellular networks, Wi-Fi, radar, satellites and billions of personal devices. Exposure has increased not incrementally, but by nine to twelve orders of magnitude (an order of magnitude is 10x), depending on how it is measured. This is not a marginal environmental change. It is a phase shift. And yet, when confronted with rising chronic illness, neurological disorders, immune dysregulation and developmental challenges, we rarely ask whether embedding biology in an alien electromagnetic environment might be part of the story. The evolutionary mismatch Biology is not a passive material system. It is a timing-sensitive, resonance-driven, coherence-seeking system . Living organisms depend on oscillations: neural rhythms, cardiac pacing, calcium signaling, hormonal pulses, circadian cycles. These processes are regulated not just by chemistry, but by phase, timing and synchronization . Life evolved in an electromagnetic environment that was: Low intensity Largely random Intermittent Non-symbolic Rich in silence There were no persistent narrowband carriers. No global phase locking. No packetized signals. No engineered repetition operating 24 hours a day. Modern EMF is qualitatively different. It is not just stronger. It is structured . Why frequency alone is the wrong question Public discussions of EMF almost always fixate on frequency and power. This leads to familiar reassurances: the frequencies are “non-ionizing,” the power levels are “below safety limits,” the energy is “too weak to break chemical bonds.” But this framing quietly assumes that biology responds primarily to energy magnitude . It does not. Biology responds to patterns . Two electromagnetic fields can share the same frequency and the same average power and yet differ radically in biological relevance depending on: Modulation Pulsing Duty cycle Repetition Phase coherence Persistence An unmodulated carrier is a smooth oscillation. A modulated signal imposes time structure — bursts, packets, envelopes and rhythms. These low-frequency envelopes often fall squarely within biological signaling ranges. From the perspective of a living system, modulation is not a technical detail. It is a qualitative feature. In simple terms: biology does not live on a spectrum analyzer. It lives in time. From noise to signal Natural electromagnetic fields are largely noise-like. They fluctuate, dissipate and leave space for recovery. Modern synthetic EMF is the opposite. It is engineered for reliability, throughput and persistence. It is optimized to eliminate randomness. This matters because living systems evolved to extract signal from noise , not to exist inside a continuous signal. Noise allows flexibility, adaptation and resilience. Persistent structured signals encourage entrainment, interference and saturation. We did not merely increase electromagnetic exposure. We transformed the informational character of the environment. Resonance: the missing variable Resonance is not an exotic concept. It is a foundational principle of physics: systems respond preferentially at certain frequencies and phase relationships. When resonance occurs, small inputs can have disproportionate effects. The human body is full of resonant systems. Neural networks synchronize. Hearts entrain. Ion channels oscillate. Membranes vibrate. These systems evolved to resonate with a quiet planetary background — not with a dense overlay of artificial oscillators operating across the spectrum. A body resonating with a natural electromagnetic environment is not equivalent to a body immersed in a synthetic frequency soup. The difference is not just quantitative. It is topological . Why mainstream research struggles here It is often said that EMF has been “extensively studied.” This is true in a narrow sense and misleading in a broader one. Most research asks questions suited to toxicology and engineering: Does it heat tissue? Is there acute damage? Is there a linear dose-response? Can we isolate one frequency and one outcome? Very little research asks the systemic question that actually matters: What happens when a complex, timing-sensitive organism is embedded for decades in a continuous, information-bearing electromagnetic environment that did not exist during its evolutionary formation? That question does not fit neatly into toxicology, physics or epidemiology. It is about coherence, regulation and long-term adaptability. It is about chronic environmental forcing , not acute injury. As a result, absence of definitive proof is routinely mistaken for absence of relevance. Illness as loss of coherence This reframing does not require claiming that EMF “causes” any single disease. That framing is too limiting. A corollary hypothesis is that chronic exposure to an engineered electromagnetic environment contributes to loss of biological coherence — reducing resilience, impairing recovery, amplifying other stressors and shifting systems toward dysregulation rather than outright damage. In such a model, EMF is not a primary toxin (which it can be in my opinion). It is a load multiplier . That would help explain why effects are subtle, variable and difficult to isolate, yet pervasive enough to matter at population scale. (There was a massive 5g rollout during the Covid Scandemic). A civilizational experiment without a control group We have conducted a planetary-scale experiment on every living organism, including ourselves. We changed the electromagnetic environment by orders of magnitude (billions to trillions of times), altered its structure, removed silence and imposed persistent artificial rhythms — all without long-term systemic study. Then we express confusion at rising illness and dysfunction. This is not an argument for panic. It is an argument for intellectual honesty . The question is no longer whether EMF is “the same as it always was.” It clearly is not. The question is whether a biology shaped by stochastic planetary fields can remain fully coherent when immersed in a dense, engineered frequency environment for a lifetime. That question deserves serious attention.
