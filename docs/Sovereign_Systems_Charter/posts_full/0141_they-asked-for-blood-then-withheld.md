## Summary
Instead, they encountered a culture that quietly revised the story: The war had been a mistake The soldiers had been naïve Their participation was morally ambiguous Their trauma was a personal malfunction, not a systemic consequence Sometimes they were thanked ceremonially but then, as we typical, dismissed substantively. The same class that urged intervention now treated those who obeyed as embarrassing reminders of its own moral failures. It cannot survive the normalization of moral conscription —the practice of demanding sacrifice from those excluded from decision-making, then denying them dignity when the bill comes due.
