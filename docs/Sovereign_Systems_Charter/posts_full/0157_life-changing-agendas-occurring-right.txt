Life Changing Agendas Occurring Right Before Our Eyes harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Life Changing Agendas Occurring Right Before Our Eyes harry blazer Dec 30, 2025 Share Most people assume that truly transformational agendas announce themselves loudly. They expect declarations, manifestos, villains or at least obvious resistance. History suggests otherwise. The most consequential shifts in how societies function tend to occur quietly, incrementally and with the reassuring language of progress, efficiency and inevitability. They are rarely framed as power grabs. They are framed as solutions. What is unfolding now belongs squarely in that category. We are constructing systems that treat all life as an engineering problem, in which humans and nature are viewed simultaneously as targets to be acted upon and constraints to be overcome. Even absent intent toward tyranny, the outcome will be the elimination of human and natural sovereignty unless explicit limits are placed on what may be prototyped, optimized, automated or owned. That sentence is not hyperbole. It is a description of trajectory. Not a conspiracy. Not anti-technology. Let me be clear at the outset. This is not an argument against technology. It is not an argument against AI. It is not partisan. And it does not require secret meetings or omnipotent masterminds. What is taking shape is something more subtle and more dangerous precisely because it does not require centralized control. It is an AI-mediated governance gestalt : an emergent environment formed by converging incentives across government, corporate, scientific, educational and social institutions, all operating under a shared belief system about optimization, efficiency and expertise. No single entity controls it. No single ideology fully owns it. Yet its direction is remarkably coherent. From systems that enforce rules to systems that replace judgment Previous technological regimes imposed rules from the outside. Laws constrained behavior. Markets shaped incentives. Surveillance punished deviation. AI-mediated systems do something fundamentally different. They do not merely enforce outcomes. They pre-structure choice itself . They decide: Which options appear Which paths are frictionless Which behaviors are rewarded or discouraged Which deviations are flagged before they occur This is not classical authoritarianism. It is ambient governance . Control does not arrive as force. It arrives as convenience. The ideology beneath the infrastructure At the heart of this convergence lies an ideology that is rarely named directly: transhumanism , not as a fringe movement but as an operating assumption. Its core belief is simple and powerful: Life can and should be optimized through engineering by those who possess the requisite knowledge and technology, rather than trusted to slow, natural processes, which cannot be owned or monetized. From that premise, everything follows: Biological limits are defects Emergence is inefficient Nature is outdated Human agency is noisy Ownership is preferable to stewardship Natural processes cannot be owned. Engineered systems can. That asymmetry alone explains the relentless push toward digitization, synthetic biology, datafication of identity and the reduction of humans to functional nodes within managed systems. POTUSes as agenda facilitators, not sovereign actors Presidents are often described as constraint managers. That is only half the picture. In practice, modern presidents function as agenda facilitators within a larger governance power grid. Their role is threefold: Clear the road by removing friction Create detours by selectively constraining unwanted flows Grease the tracks for the flows deemed desirable This does not require personal belief in an ideology. It requires functional alignment. Trump’s role in the current moment is best understood this way. Within days of taking office, he participated in highly visible announcements aligning federal power with massive AI-related infrastructure investment, framed as a national security necessity. At the same time, executive actions that could have introduced friction or ethical hesitation were removed, while federal initiatives advanced that would undermine state-level resistance. These actions matter not because of Trump’s inner convictions, but because they accelerate lock-in . Infrastructure once built does not ask permission. It demands use. To be clear, this trajectory is bipartisan. Rhetoric changes. Tone changes. Personnel changes. The direction largely does not. Why intent does not matter as much as structure One of the most persistent misunderstandings in this debate is the assumption that tyranny requires tyrants. It does not any longer. Systems capable of total mediation do not need malicious intent to produce domination. They require only: Incentives aligned toward efficiency Risk aversion institutionalized Accountability diffused Convenience rewarded Once such systems exist, the pressure to use them becomes overwhelming. What can be optimized will be optimized. What can be automated will be automated. What can be owned will be owned. Not because anyone is evil (though some are) but because the system rewards those who comply with its logic. Sovereignty as a boundary condition, not a preference Human and natural sovereignty are often framed as sentimental values, luxuries that must give way to progress. That framing is wrong. Sovereignty is a boundary condition . It is what prevents complex systems from collapsing into total control architectures. It preserves the irreducible, the unquantifiable and the non-optimizable. When sovereignty is treated as expendable, life itself becomes provisional. The critical question before us is not whether AI will advance. It will. The question is whether we define explicit limits before optimization becomes synonymous with ownership and automation becomes synonymous with authority. What is at stake Once systems capable of total mediation are normalized, reversing them becomes nearly impossible. Resistance is reframed as inefficiency and inconvenience. Dissent becomes noise. Autonomy becomes a bug. The most dangerous agendas are not those announced with force, but those implemented quietly, rationally and with the full confidence of experts who believe they know better. These are life-changing agendas. They are unfolding in plain sight. And the window to define what must never be optimized is closing.
