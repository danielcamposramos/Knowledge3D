Popper: From Certainty to Conjecture We then moved to Karl Popper , who rejected the quest for certainty entirely. Instead of asking “How can I know that I am right?” Popper asked “How can I know when I am wrong?” His answer: through falsifiability . We contrasted this with Descartes’ rationalism: Descartes wanted to establish truth beyond doubt; Popper replaces the will to certainty with a method of humility . Popper “turns the epistemic impulse inside out.” Instead of seeking foundations, he builds a ladder of provisional hypotheses , always open to revision. But we also discussed the paradox of Popper’s stance : even falsifiability depends on linguistic and methodological frameworks that themselves are not falsifiable. In the Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus , Wittgenstein still shared with Descartes and the logical positivists the dream of precision: that the structure of language mirrors the structure of reality. But the book ends with the haunting line: “Whereof one cannot speak, thereof one must be silent.” We saw that this is both a warning and a revelation — the boundary where meaning ends . The world can be pictured, but not the relationship between the picture and the world. Ethics, aesthetics and the self fall outside the sayable. Meaning arises not from correspondence but from use — from participation in “language games.” Truth, in this view, is not discovered but enacted within human forms of life. We noted that Wittgenstein’s late philosophy effectively dissolves Descartes’ and Popper’s problems alike: Descartes sought certainty beyond language. Popper sought refutation within scientific language. Wittgenstein realized that both are prisoners of (dependent on) language itself . 4.
