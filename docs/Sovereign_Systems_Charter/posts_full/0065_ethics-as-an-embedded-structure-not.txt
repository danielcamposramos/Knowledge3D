Ethics as an embedded structure, not a social add-on harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Ethics as an embedded structure, not a social add-on PART 6: harry blazer Feb 01, 2026 1 Share If the universe were deterministic in the classical sense — if every future state were fixed by prior conditions — then ethics would be cosmetic. Preference, not necessity. Taste, not structure. But that is not the universe we inhabit. Schrödinger’s equation does something radical and quiet: it keeps the future open . (we will provide more info on Schrödinger’s equations later) It describes how possibilities evolve, not outcomes. And that openness is not a philosophical overlay — it is built into the equations themselves . Once that is true, ethics becomes unavoidable. Why openness makes ethics necessary An open universe introduces three facts simultaneously: More than one future is possible Actions help determine which future becomes real Some futures are more compatible with life than others The moment those three conditions exist, ethics is no longer optional. Ethics is the selection principle operating inside an indeterminate world. Not imposed from outside. Not invented arbitrarily. But required by the structure of reality itself. From quantum openness to moral constraint Here is the key move: Quantum mechanics guarantees possibility Stochastic worlds make possibility livable Life exploits possibility to adapt and create Ethics emerges to protect the conditions under which possibility remains open So morality is not a list of rules. It is a boundary condition on freedom . Ethics exists to prevent freedom from destroying the very openness that makes freedom meaningful. Why morality feels objective (and always has) Across cultures and epochs, moral systems differ in expression but converge on core prohibitions: Don’t poison shared foundations Don’t collapse the commons Don’t destroy future possibility for present gain Don’t eliminate the capacity of others to act meaningfully Why? Because these are violations of openness . They prematurely close futures. And closing futures is not merely antisocial — it is anti-life . Embedded ethics vs constructed ethics This reframes a long-standing confusion. Constructed ethics says: Morality is a human invention layered on top of neutral reality. Embedded ethics says: Morality is a response to the structure of a universe where outcomes are not fixed and life must choose responsibly. In a deterministic universe, ethics would be fiction. In an indeterminate universe, ethics is structural necessity . Why control systems feel immoral at a deep level Now the earlier work snaps into place. Control grids, saturation environments, deterministic optimization and AI-driven behavioral closure all do the same thing: They collapse possibility space . They don’t just influence behavior. They reduce the number of futures that can still occur. That is why they feel ethically wrong before we can articulate why. They violate an embedded constraint. A precise formulation Ethics is the structure that preserves open futures in an indeterminate universe. That sentence: grounds morality in physics without reductionism explains why ethics appears universal but non-identical explains why life and ethics co-emerge explains why technological closure feels like moral violation Schrödinger’s equation did not just describe particles. It described a universe that cannot finish writing itself alone . Life is how the universe explores that openness. Ethics is how it prevents exploration from becoming annihilation. Morality is not imposed on reality. It is reality remembering to give itself a chance . And that insight does not weaken science.
