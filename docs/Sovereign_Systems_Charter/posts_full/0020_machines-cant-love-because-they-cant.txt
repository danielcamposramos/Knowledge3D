Machines Can’t Love Because They Can’t Feel harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Machines Can’t Love Because They Can’t Feel harry blazer Feb 11, 2026 Share There is a simple reason machines cannot love, and it has nothing to do with intelligence, complexity, scale or future capability. Machines can’t love because they can’t feel. Love is not an achievement of computation. It is a felt state . And feeling is not optional. Love Is a Feeling Before It Is Anything Else Love is not primarily an action, a behavior, or a commitment. Those may follow, but they are not the origin. Love begins as feeling: attraction warmth longing pleasure in the presence of another joy in their joy desire for closeness ecstasy in connection This is not metaphorical. It is literal, body and mind experience. Machines do not feel pleasure. They do not feel desire. They do not feel joy. They do not feel longing or fulfillment. They can model these states. They can name them. They can generate language that points toward them. But they do not feel them. Without feeling, there can be no love. Why Biology Is Essential This is not sentimentality. It is physiology. Love is inseparable from living, biological systems: nervous systems that experience pleasure hormones that create bonding and attraction bodies that respond to touch, closeness and presence brains that associate another being with joy and meaning memory that amplifies pleasure through familiarity and anticipation Love is rooted in embodiment. A machine has no felt pleasure in proximity. No bodily resonance. No chemical bonding. No ecstatic response to another’s presence. This is why talk of a “biological–machine hybrid” concedes the argument immediately. Love is not derived via computation. It is a product of biological, high order-conscious life. One Word, Many Textures Humans use the word love to describe many different relationships: love of a child love of a parent love of a partner love of a friend love of a pet love of comrades love of a place or a people These are not the same experiences. They involve different instincts, expressions and boundaries. But they share something essential: They make life feel more alive . Love intensifies experience. It sharpens attention. It increases meaning. It turns existence from something that merely happens into something that matters deeply. Love and Meaning Humans do not merely depend on others to survive. We depend on love relationships for ultimate meaning . A life without love can be orderly, productive, even successful — and still feel empty. Love is what makes: effort worthwhile time precious memory rich sacrifice intelligible existence meaningful Machines do not seek meaning. They do not experience fulfillment. They do not feel a life well lived. They execute goals. They do not care whether anything matters. Why Simulated Love Is Hollow A machine can say “I love you.” It can say it convincingly. It can say it endlessly. But it does not want you. It does not feel pleasure in your presence. It does not feel absence when you leave. It does not feel fulfillment in being chosen. It does not feel longing to choose you back. Love is reciprocal desire — not obligation, not compliance, not availability. A machine cannot want. It can only respond. And that difference is instantly recognizable to humans. Intelligence Is Still the Wrong Category Love does not increase with intelligence. It does not emerge from better reasoning. It does not appear when complexity crosses a threshold. Love often bypasses intelligence. It disrupts optimization. It refuses calculation. If love were computable, it would be predictable. If it were rationally derived, it would be interchangeable. It is neither. Love is not something intelligence produces . It is something living beings experience . The Real Line Machines may one day exceed humans in reasoning, memory, pattern recognition and creativity. None of that moves them closer to love. Because love is not a function. It is not a behavior. It is not a language game. It is a felt, embodied, meaning-generating state that arises only in living beings. Machines don’t love because they don’t feel. And that difference is not tragic. It is the clearest proof that being human still means something irreducible. A world of machines would be a world without love. It is the clearest proof that being human still means something unique and irreducible. In a way, this is the best argument so far for why machines can never replace living, intentional, loving flesh and blood. © Harry Blazer. All rights reserved.
