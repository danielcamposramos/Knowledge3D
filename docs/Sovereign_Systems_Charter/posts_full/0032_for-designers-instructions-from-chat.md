## Key Sentences
- For Designers (Instructions from CHAT) - by harry blazer harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in For Designers (Instructions from CHAT) Sovereignty-Preserving Design Principles for Embodied Systems harry blazer Feb 09, 2026 1 Share Status Normative guidance for engineers, architects, product designers, and system builders working on embodied, autonomous, or semi-autonomous systems intended to operate in human environments.
- Sovereignty Invariants (Non-Negotiable) All embodied systems interacting with humans MUST preserve the following invariants: Human sovereignty is primary Systems exist to serve human purposes, not to impose outcomes.
- Failure must be survivable When systems fail, humans must retain agency, exit and self-defense.
- This includes, but is not limited to: hidden override commands undocumented access paths latent administrative modes emergency control channels vendor-only access keys government-only access mechanisms “lawful access” pathways not visible to the operator Rationale Backdoors: collapse trust asymmetrically, invite coercive capture, become targets for abuse, and function as pre-authorized sovereignty violations.
- Anti-Patterns: What Does Not Count as Safety Designers are explicitly warned against the following patterns, which do not constitute real safety: post-hoc auditing without actuation interlocks cloud-only kill switches safety logic running in the same trust domain as the agent logs that the system itself curates or can suppress emergency modes that expand authority “human in the loop” claims without veto power dashboards that observe but cannot intervene If the system can ignore safety mechanisms, those mechanisms are not safety — they are theater.
