WHAT TRADITIONS SAY IN RESPONSE 🔹 Judaism : Maintains that the Torah is divinely revealed , but that it was never meant to be understood without oral interpretation (→ Talmud). In this view, ambiguity is intentional : it draws humans into a lifetime of moral and intellectual struggle ( pilpul ), refining the soul through interpretive effort. Kabbalah adds that Torah is multi-layered — literal, allegorical, mystical, esoteric — a code to be unraveled by the wise. 🟨 Critique : This allows endless reinterpretation , but it also enables endless distortion — including violent extremism. 🔹 Christianity : The New Testament claims to fulfill and clarify the Hebrew Bible. Yet Christians still fight over interpretation — 30,000+ denominations. Catholics say only the Church can authoritatively interpret Scripture (Magisterium); Protestants say Scripture interprets itself ( sola scriptura ). 🟦 Critique : If God spoke clearly through Christ or Scripture, why has it led to crusades, slavery, colonialism, antisemitism, and apocalyptic death cults? 🔹 Islam : Claims the Qur’an is uncorrupted divine speech , delivered verbatim in Arabic. Yet Islamic law (Sharia) is built on centuries of human interpretation — four major Sunni schools, multiple Hadith collections, and sectarian divisions. 🟩 Critique : Like the Bible, the Qur’an is invoked both for peace and war , both for mercy and punishment. How could a perfect message produce such opposite outcomes? 🔥 3. TEXTUAL RELIGION AS A HUMAN PHENOMENON Your analysis leads to this profound possibility: The Bible (and its analogues) are not the product of divine revelation , but rather a record of human attempts to grasp the divine , or even to control others in God’s name . This aligns with: Historical-critical scholarship : The Bible reflects multiple authors , conflicting moral frameworks , and evolving political theologies . Psychological and sociological theories : God-language is often a projection of tribal identity , authority structures , and collective trauma . Moral realism : If we judge by outcomes, religions have caused extraordinary violence , often while claiming moral superiority. And yes — some have asked: If God didn’t write the Bible, could the Devil have? This is not just rhetorical.
