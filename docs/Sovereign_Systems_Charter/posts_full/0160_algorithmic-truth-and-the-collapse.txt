Algorithmic Truth and the Collapse of Reality harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Algorithmic Truth and the Collapse of Reality harry blazer Dec 28, 2025 2 Share We are told we live in an age of information abundance. What we actually live in is an age of algorithmic selection . This distinction matters — because selection is power. In recent weeks, I watched a strange phenomenon unfold around a public figure in sports. False claims circulated freely: she had quit, defected, been elevated, been punished, been erased. None of it was true. Corrections were available, simple and verifiable. Yet the lies propagated faster, farther and more persistently than the truth. This alone is remarkable in itself. What is more remarkable is the contrast. During Covid, truth was aggressively suppressed in the name of “misinformation.” Platforms intervened, censored, de-platformed, memory-holed and throttled dissenting voices — including credentialed experts — with breathtaking confidence. Now, in a context where no institutional risk exists, outright falsehoods are allowed to spread unchecked. This contradiction exposes the real operating principle of our digital world. The Myth of the Neutral Platform Google, YouTube and their peers are not truth systems. They are optimization systems . Their primary objectives are not accuracy, coherence or correspondence with reality. They are: Engagement Watch time Click-through rate Emotional intensity Session duration Truth is not part of the objective function. In fact, truth often performs poorly. Truth closes loops. Truth resolves uncertainty. Truth dampens outrage. Algorithms prefer open loops — narratives that provoke, polarize and persist. So the system does not ask: Is this true? It asks: Does this propagate? Why Lies Outperform Truth Falsehoods have evolutionary advantages in algorithmic environments. They are: Simpler More absolute More emotionally activating More identity-affirming Truth, by contrast, is conditional. It requires context, restraint and sometimes patience. In a system that rewards velocity and intensity, distortion is not an aberration — it is a competitive adaptation. Creators learn this quickly. They adjust headlines, framing and claims to match what the algorithm rewards. Corrections underperform. Nuance underperforms. Retractions underperform. The system selects against epistemic discipline. Why Covid Was Different Many people still believe censorship during Covid was about “preventing misinformation.” It was not. It was about risk containment . Covid-era truth threatened: Government authority Institutional credibility Legal liability Pharmaceutical alignment Public compliance That created vertical risk — risk upward , toward power. In response, platforms temporarily overrode engagement optimization in favor of institutional risk management. Now compare that to sports, culture or celebrity misinformation. These narratives: Threaten no centralized power Generate massive engagement Create horizontal conflict (people vs people) Carry no regulatory or legal exposure So they are allowed — even rewarded. The rule is simple: Truth is suppressed when it threatens power. Lies are tolerated because they increase market share, even if they fracture the public. Algorithmic Truth Is Not Truth We have crossed a dangerous threshold. Truth is no longer defined by evidence, correspondence or refutation. It is defined by algorithmic permission . What is “true” is what is allowed to circulate. What is “false” is what is throttled or erased. This is not censorship in the old sense. It is worse. It is epistemic corrosion by optimization . No editor decides. No censor signs an order. No accountable human takes responsibility. The system simply “learns” what keeps us engaged — and reality bends accordingly. Power Without Accountability (Again) This pattern mirrors what we see everywhere else: In finance, where risk is socialized and gains privatized In law, where power is decoupled from consequence In technology, where systems act without moral agency In governance, where opacity replaces consent Algorithms now exercise power over perception without accountability for truth. They shape what billions believe, fear and argue about — while claiming neutrality. That claim is false. Selection is agency. Optimization is intention. Outcomes are not accidental. Why This Matters A society cannot remain sovereign if it cannot distinguish signal from noise. When truth is flattened into content, when lies outperform facts, when clarity is penalized and outrage rewarded, people lose their ability to orient themselves in reality. Confusion becomes stabilizing. Clarity becomes destabilizing. That is not an accident. A confused public is governable. A discerning public is not. The Deeper Pattern Digitization compresses the possible into the measurable. Algorithms compress reality into what can be optimized. But truth is not always efficient. Human judgment is not always coherent. Meaning does not always scale. This is why analog sovereignty matters. This is why human discernment cannot be delegated. This is why systems that optimize without moral constraint inevitably corrode the very reality they claim to organize. Final Word We are not suffering from a lack of information. We are suffering from information selected against truth . Until power is re-coupled to accountability — epistemic, moral and institutional — the system will continue to reward distortion and punish clarity. Seeing this clearly is the first act of sovereignty.
