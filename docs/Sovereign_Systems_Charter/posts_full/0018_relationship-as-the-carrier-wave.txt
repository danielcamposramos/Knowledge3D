Relationship as the Carrier Wave of Meaning harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Relationship as the Carrier Wave of Meaning harry blazer Feb 11, 2026 Share There comes a moment — often quietly, without drama — when an otherwise competent, thinking, autonomous person realizes something unsettling: Life can function perfectly well without meaning. Systems run. Days pass. Needs are met. Problems are solved. Goals are achieved. And yet, something essential is missing. That absence is not comfort, pleasure or even happiness. It is meaning . And meaning, it turns out, is not something we generate alone. Meaning Is Not an Internal Property Modern culture teaches that meaning is an individual construction: values chosen, goals selected, identity asserted. This view flatters autonomy, but it quietly collapses under lived experience. Meaning does not behave like a private possession. It behaves like a field . An achievement unseen feels thinner. A joy unshared fades faster. A sorrow carried alone becomes corrosive. This is not sentimentality. It is structural. Meaning emerges between conscious beings, not inside them. Relationship as the Carrier Wave of Meaning Relationships are not merely emotional attachments or social conveniences. They are the carrier wave that allows experience to register as significant. A life event becomes meaningful when: it is shared it is witnessed it is remembered it is reflected back it is held across time by another mind Without this, even extraordinary experiences degrade into isolated data points. The human psyche does not metabolize reality in isolation. It requires resonance. This is why people instinctively tell stories. Why grief seeks company. Why love insists on presence. Not because humans are weak — but because meaning is relational by nature . Identity Is Co-Created, Not Declared We like to say “I know who I am.” But that knowledge is incomplete without relational feedback. Others reveal us to ourselves: through resistance through care through misunderstanding through recognition Even conflict contributes to identity formation. Total isolation, by contrast, dissolves selfhood into abstraction. One becomes coherent, but not grounded. This is why enforced loneliness is psychologically destructive, and why chosen solitude can be restorative: one erodes identity, the other presupposes it. Love Turns Time into Narrative Without relationships, time becomes a sequence. With relationships, time becomes a story. Stories require continuity, memory and shared reference. Love — romantic, familial, or chosen — stitches moments together into something that persists beyond the present instant. This is why relationships survive inconvenience, distance, frustration and imperfection. They are not optimized because they are not transactional. They are meaning-preserving structures . The Systemic Threat to Relationship Here is where the insight sharpens. Modern systems — economic, technological, bureaucratic — increasingly reward: speed over presence efficiency over attentiveness abstraction over embodiment scalability over intimacy Relationships resist all four. They are slow. They are inefficient. They are irreducibly analog. They do not scale. And because they do not scale, they are quietly deprioritized — framed as optional, private or sentimental rather than foundational. This is not accidental. A population oriented around relationship is harder to abstract, harder to optimize and harder to control. Why Relationships Cannot Be Replaced No system can replace the experience of being known . Not because systems are insufficiently advanced, but because meaning requires: mutual vulnerability unoptimized presence moral recognition accountability between agents who can refuse These properties cannot be simulated without collapsing into performance. An imitation of relationship can satisfy habit, but it cannot generate meaning. This is why people continue to reach for human connection even when it is risky, inefficient or painful. They are not chasing comfort. They are defending meaning. The Deeper Realization What “hits” when this realization arrives is not that relationships are nice or helpful. It is this: A life without relationship may be coherent, functional and even successful — but it is ontologically thin. Meaning does not live in systems. It does not live in optimization. It does not live in autonomy alone. It lives where one conscious being encounters another and neither can be reduced to function. That space is sacred not because it is perfect, but because it is real . And it is the one domain no machine, no institution and no abstraction can rightfully replace. © Harry Blazer.
