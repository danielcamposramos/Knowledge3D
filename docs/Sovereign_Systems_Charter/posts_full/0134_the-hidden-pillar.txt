They do so through some of the most sophisticated legal talent ever assembled — people whose brilliance lies in making harm survivable for institutions, even as it becomes permanent for everyone else. What These Law Firms Actually Do (Beyond “Defense”) The popular image — lawyers defending clients after the fact — is incomplete. The real work happens upstream . Elite firms specialize in four functions that map perfectly onto the immunity pattern we’ve identified: A. Pre-liability architecture Structuring entities before harm occurs Separating operating companies from IP holders Designing spin-offs that absorb future liability (Chemours is the textbook case) B. Statutory co-creation Drafting legislative language Advising Congressional committees Shaping “must-pass” provisions during crisis windows Translating industry needs into neutral legal phrasing This is how Price–Anderson , VICP , and PREP-style immunities are born. C. Regulatory choreography Managing agency rulemaking Creating compliance regimes that substitute for accountability Turning “best practices” into legal shields D. Temporal risk diffusion Structuring settlements over decades Using trusts, funds, and capped pools Ensuring liability never converges in one moment or entity Law is used to bend time , not just rules. The Firms That Specialize in This Work We do not need to allege conspiracy. We simply need to show who repeatedly appears where catastrophic risk meets state power. Examples of firms historically and structurally embedded in this terrain include: Covington & Burling Deep ties to pharma, regulatory agencies, national security and emergency authorities Sidley Austin Major role in administrative law, health, energy, and crisis governance Skadden Arps Corporate restructuring, spin-offs, liability partitioning Kirkland & Ellis Private equity, restructuring, risk insulation at scale Latham & Watkins Energy, infrastructure, regulatory capture pathways The point is that this expertise exists, is concentrated, is strategic as well as repeatedly mobilized at moments of systemic risk .
