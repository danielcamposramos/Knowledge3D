From World Wars to World Management: The Long Arc of Treachery harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in From World Wars to World Management: The Long Arc of Treachery harry blazer Jan 03, 2026 4 Share The greatest deception of the twentieth century was not any single war. It was the belief that the wars were discrete , reactive and morally resolved . They were not. World War II did not erupt from nowhere, nor did it redeem the treacheries of World War I. It completed them . World War I: The Betrayal That Set the Pattern World War I was sold to populations as: Defensive Inevitable Brief It was none of those things. It was the first fully industrialized war, and the first war in which financial systems, arms manufacturers and imperial banking interests discovered that prolonged conflict was not a tragedy to be avoided, but a mechanism to be optimized. Young men were fed into trenches while debts were refinanced, borders redrawn and empires reorganized. When the war ended, it did not end in justice. It ended in punitive financial architecture —reparations, debt servitude and humiliation—laid deliberately atop a shattered Europe. The conditions for the next war were not an accident. They were installed . World War II: The Managed Inferno By the 1930s, the system had learned. Total war was no longer simply fought. It was managed . This is not a denial of real evil. Nazism was real, murderous and totalitarian. Millions died at its hands. That truth stands. What is less comfortable is this: the rise, consolidation and timing of the conflict were shaped by forces that did not disappear when the war ended . Certain industrial firms profited on both sides of the conflict Financial channels continued operating across nominal enemy lines Intelligence services selectively ignored or exploited foreknowledge Opportunism became a new cultural ethic. Morality was declared conditional. Civilian populations were subjected to mass psychological conditioning The war mobilized entire societies—but decision-making remained tightly centralized , insulated from popular consent or comprehension. Again, the pattern held: The many fought The few directed The few did not bleed The Postwar Illusion World War II is remembered as the good war because it produced a clean narrative: evil defeated, peace restored, democracy triumphant. But what actually followed was not peace. It was institutional permanence : Permanent military-industrial capacity Permanent intelligence apparatuses Permanent emergency justification Permanent debt expansion The war ended, but the architecture of war did not demobilize . It metastasized. From Kinetic War to Civilizational War This brings us to the present. This war is not primarily fought with bombs and battalions—though those still exist, as enforcement. It is fought with: Digitization Surveillance Debt Algorithmic control Psychological operations Narrative management This war does not merely seek territory. It seeks sovereignty itself . Not just national sovereignty—but human sovereignty: The right to choose The right to refuse The right to remain analog The right to be irreducible to data And yes—this war is prosecuted for profit . Not metaphorically. Literally. Every layer of control is monetized. Every compliance pathway is capitalized. Every crisis becomes a revenue stream. The Continuity of the Forces A discernible pattern The actors change. The methods evolve. The language softens. But the logic is continuous from WWI to WWII to the Cold War to today: Centralize power Externalize sacrifice Abstract consequence Monetize control The soldiers of earlier wars lost limbs, minds and lives. The targets of this war risk losing agency, meaning and the capacity for refusal . In a word sovereignty . Why This Is the Real Battlefield This is not nostalgia. This is not denial of past heroism. This is not disrespect to those who fought. It is respect. Because honoring sacrifice means refusing to let it be endlessly recycled as fuel for systems that no longer even pretend to serve humanity.
