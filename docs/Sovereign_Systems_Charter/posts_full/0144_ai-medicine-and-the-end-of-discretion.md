- AI systems are trained on: Population datasets Coded diagnoses Institutional outcomes Billing and compliance structures They learn what works on average , under defined conditions , for most people .
- The Sovereignty Collision Individual sovereignty depends on: The legitimacy of subjective experience The right to refuse protocol The authority to act on early signals The freedom to deviate without penalty Algorithmic medicine depends on: Predictability Standardization Auditability Compliance These are not morally opposed.
- The Necessary Boundary If AI is to be used ethically in medicine, certain principles must be non-negotiable: Human discretion must remain protected Protocols must be defeasible, not binding Early subjective signals must retain legitimacy Opt-out pathways must exist without penalty Without these, medicine will become more coherent — and less humane.
- The future of medicine will not be decided by algorithms alone — but by whether individuals are still allowed to say: “This may be safe on average, but it is not safe for me.” If that sentence becomes unsayable, sovereignty will have been lost.
