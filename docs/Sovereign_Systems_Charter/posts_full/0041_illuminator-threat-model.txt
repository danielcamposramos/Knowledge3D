ILLUMINATOR: Threat Model - by harry blazer harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in ILLUMINATOR: Threat Model Part 4: Resilience, Capture Resistance and Survival of the Illuminator harry blazer Feb 06, 2026 Share 1. Purpose The purpose of this document is to enumerate, without illusion, the ways in which a moral illumination system will be attacked, captured, distorted or suppressed , and to define structural countermeasures that preserve its function. The Illuminator is not neutral. It threatens opacity, impunity and moral laundering. It must therefore be designed to survive hostility . 2. Core Assumption The Illuminator must assume: sustained opposition from powerful actors attempts at co-option disguised as partnership legal, financial and narrative attacks internal corrosion over time pressure to “moderate,” “balance,” or “simplify” Failure to assume this guarantees failure. 3. Threat Classes 3.1 Political Suppression Threats classification creep national security pretexts emergency powers jurisdictional shutdowns export control or sanctions pressure Countermeasures operate exclusively on public-domain material distribute mirrors across multiple jurisdictions avoid single-country incorporation maintain stateless publication nodes publish methodology so suppression appears as censorship 3.2 Legal Warfare (Lawfare) Threats defamation suits injunctions discovery abuse regulatory harassment forced disclosure of contributors Countermeasures no claims of intent, guilt or illegality evidence-only reporting precise language discipline institutional focus only (no individuals) separation between contributors and publication infrastructure redundancy of legal entities 3.3 Financial Capture Threats dependency on single donors funding conditionality “philanthropic alignment” sponsorship pressure monetization creep Countermeasures diversified, capped funding sources transparent financial disclosures refusal of conditional funding operational frugality by design no advertising, sponsorships or endorsements Money must never become leverage. 3.4 Narrative & Reputation Attacks Threats labeling as extremist, conspiratorial or foreign-influenced character assassination of contributors false equivalence framing (“both sides”) deliberate misinterpretation of alarms saturation with noise and distraction Countermeasures radical methodological transparency disciplined, unemotional tone evidence-forward publication refusal to engage in identity defense letting institutions speak through their own documents The system does not argue. It shows. 3.5 Technical Attacks Threats denial-of-service hosting takedowns data corruption supply-chain compromise model poisoning Countermeasures distributed hosting and mirrors cryptographic integrity checks reproducible builds open-source pipelines independent verification nodes offline archival releases Availability is multiplied, not defended. 3.6 Internal Corrosion Threats mission drift moral inflation scope creep ego capture “activist” pressure fatigue and normalization Countermeasures explicit scope boundaries constitutionally enforced limits periodic principle audits visible dissent and minority reports rotation of stewardship roles formal refusal pathways The greatest long-term threat is internal. 3.7 AI-Specific Risks Threats model bias shaping alarms over-reliance on automation hidden training data influence pressure to “smooth” outputs silent suppression of edge cases Countermeasures human-in-the-loop review multiple model comparison disclosure of model limitations preservation of raw evidence alarms cannot be auto-suppressed AI outputs are advisory only AI assists illumination. It does not own it. 4. Decapitation Resistance The Illuminator must not be stoppable by: arresting a founder discrediting a leader seizing a server freezing a bank account revoking a license Structural Requirements no single spokesperson no single legal home no single technical stack no single funding rail no single governance node If one instance falls, others persist. 5. Red Lines (Non-Negotiables) If any of the following occur, the system must refuse continuation rather than compromise: monitoring of private individuals enforcement authority secret criteria or scoring hidden funding opaque algorithms partnership requiring silence suppression of alarms by authority Better to fail openly than succeed corruptly. 6. What the Illuminator Is Not To prevent category drift, the Illuminator explicitly rejects being framed as: a regulator a court a police force a rating agency for individuals a social credit system a political movement a revolutionary vanguard It is a moral observatory , not a power center. 7. Measure of Success Success is not : adoption by governments endorsement by corporations mainstream approval influence over policy Success is: persistent visibility of moral incoherence inability to erase evidence normalization of public moral accounting restoration of pause where speed once ruled If powerful actors are uncomfortable — the system is working. 8. Final Principle When systems cannot be trusted to restrain themselves, illumination becomes the last non-violent corrective. The Illuminator exists to make moral abdication undeniable — not to stop it by force, but to ensure it cannot hide. Status This threat model is foundational . Any implementation that cannot meet it should not proceed. Survival without integrity is not survival. © Harry Blazer. All rights reserved.
