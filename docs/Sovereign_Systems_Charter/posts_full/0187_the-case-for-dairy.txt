THE CASE FOR DAIRY - by harry blazer - harry’s Substack harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in THE CASE FOR DAIRY Biology, History, Culture and Sovereignty harry blazer Dec 06, 2025 1 4 Share There is a fashionable modern belief that adult humans have no business consuming milk, that dairy is “unnatural,” “non-evolutionary” or somehow a biological mistake. This argument sounds scientific. It even borrows evolutionary language. But it collapses under the slightest scrutiny. The truth is simpler and far more interesting: real dairy has nourished human beings for thousands of years, shaped entire civilizations and remains one of the most nutrient-dense foods available to adults today. To understand this, we need to separate two very different things: Industrial dairy — pasteurized, homogenized, A1-dominant, antibiotic-saturated, confined-animal liquid. Ancestral dairy — raw, grass-fed, full-fat milk from cattle, goats, sheep, buffalo, yak and other ruminants that convert sunlight and grass into life-giving nutrition. Only the first deserves criticism. The second is one of the most profound, ancient human-food relationships in existence. Milk Is Not an Evolutionary Accident — Lactase Persistence Is Evolution in Action Around 10,000 years ago, certain human populations developed a mutation that allowed the lactase enzyme — which typically declines after infancy — to remain active. This was not random. This was selection pressure . Groups that could digest dairy gained: stable calories complete protein saturated fat bioavailable minerals essential vitamins immune factors clean hydration In environments where crops failed or hunting fluctuated, dairy meant survival, fertility, cognitive development and population stability . Lactase persistence spread because it conferred an advantage . That is evolution. The anti-milk crowd inverts the logic: they treat adaptation as pathology and adaptation’s benefits as irrelevant (unless it serves their agenda). A species that can turn grass — essentially cellulose — into bio-intelligent calories is a biological miracle. A species that can harvest that miracle through milk is equally remarkable. This is sovereignty. This is human ingenuity. Dairy Culture As Human Culture Name a high-altitude, cold-climate or marginal-land civilization and you will find dairy at its heart: Tibetans with yak milk Maasai with cow blood and milk Mongols with fermented mare’s milk Alpine Europeans with cow, goat and sheep cheese The Sami with reindeer milk Steppe peoples with cultured dairy as their primary staple These cultures didn’t just consume dairy. They thrived on it. Yak milk alone is responsible for the survival of numerous Himalayan populations. It supplies fat, protein and micronutrients unavailable from plant foods at altitude. Buffalo milk sustained huge parts of South Asia. Goat and sheep milk fed the Mediterranean world long before global grain agriculture. Dairy was not supplemental. It was central. Would Our Ancestors Drink Milk From a Killed Animal? The idea that hunter-gatherers would ignore a lactating animal they had just taken down is absurd. The udder contains fat, sugar, protein, growth factors, immune complexes and hydration. They would have consumed it first. This is not speculation. Ethnographic records confirm it. The modern claim that “no other animal drinks milk as an adult” misses the obvious point: no other animal has the cognitive capacity, technology, cooperative structure or relationship with ruminants to cultivate milk. That is not a criticism. That is a celebration of human intelligence and animal/human symbiosis. Real Dairy Is a Fermented Food, a Fat Food and a Survival Food Milk has many forms: raw milk yogurt kefir cheese clotted cream butter ghee Each one concentrates or transforms nutrients differently. All support the microbiome, digestion and immune function. Cheese alone is one of the earliest human technologies for nutrient preservation. The question is not whether humans should consume dairy in adulthood. The question is whether they should consume real dairy , not industrial imitationst - including sad to say, ultra-pasteurized “organic” dairy. Dairy, done right, remains: nutrient dense hormone supportive metabolically stabilizing immune supportive evolutionarily consistent civilizationally foundational Adult humans don’t just tolerate dairy. A great many of us were shaped by it . As with animal fat, the demonization of dairy has almost nothing to do with biology and everything to do with modern ideology, profit structures and the industrial worldview that despises any food source it cannot control or patent. Rebuttal to Anti-Milk Talking Points \ 1. “Humans are the only animals that drink milk as adults.” Response: Humans are also the only animals that build houses, write symphonies, make tools, plant crops and ferment foods. Human uniqueness is not an argument against milk. It is an argument for our adaptive intelligence. Other mammals drink milk as long as it is available. Adults do not drink it simply because no other species has invented dairying. 2. “We didn’t evolve to drink milk.” Response: False. Lactase persistence is one of the best-studied evolutionary adaptations of the last 10,000 years. It emerged precisely because milk was a survival advantage. Evolution rewarded dairy consumption with increased reproductive success. Adaptation is evolution. 3. “Milk is inflammatory.” Response: Industrial A1, homogenized, pasteurized, ultra-pasteurized milk can be inflammatory. Real A2, grass-fed, raw or fermented dairy rarely is. You shouldn’t judge beef by industrial feedlot beef. You shouldn’t judge dairy by industrial milk. 4. “Adults don’t need milk.” Response: Adults don’t need houses, clothes, tools or cooked food either. Yet all of these profoundly improve survival. Milk provides: calcium in bioavailable form K2 (in aged cheeses) vitamin A full-spectrum protein healthy saturated fats immune components probiotics (when fermented) It is one of the most complete foods on earth. 5. “Milk is for babies.” Response: True — and human adults are the only species smart enough to preserve access to that nutrient-rich substance by domesticating ruminants. That is not a flaw. That is exceptionalism in action. 6. “Hunter-gatherers didn’t drink milk.” Response: Some did, especially transitional or pastoral groups. And all hunter-gatherers would have consumed udder milk from a kill without hesitation. The anti-milk position relies on a fantasy of the past that never existed. 7. “Dairy is unnatural.” Response: There is nothing more natural than a ruminant converting grass into fat-rich, protein-rich, vitamin and mineral-rich milk. It is one of the most elegant biological systems on earth. What is unnatural is powdered plant or ultra-pasteurized slurry and all the other industrial concoctions posing as “milk.” 8. “Milk consumption is harmful to the planet.” Response: This argument ignores the role of marginal land — regions where crops can’t grow but ruminants can thrive. Cattle, goats, sheep, yak, and buffalo upcycle unusable cellulose into dense human nutrition. Eliminating them would increase ecological fragility, not reduce it. The methane argument is scientifically unserious. Ruminants are part of the carbon cycle, not a novel disturbance. Bottom Line The anti-milk ideology is not nutritional science. It is: industrial propaganda anti-animal agricultural politics climate fear-mongering vegan moralism corporate positioning for patented “milk substitutes” a systematic erasure of one of humanity’s most successful food relationships Real dairy supports: fertility immunity bone health cognitive development metabolic stability civilizational resilience And perhaps most importantly, it supports sovereignty — agricultural, biological and cultural. 1 4 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks Carmen Lilly Dec 6 I really like your work and in no way want to be negative as a Holistic practitioner with 30 years experience ..unfortunately Milk products are a big cause of allergic and inflammatory burden that disrupt the gut and Immune system and feed pathogens..in my experience ..and many people have multiple symptoms caused by them ...even after detoxing and repairing the gut Reply Share 3 replies by harry blazer and others 3 more comments...
