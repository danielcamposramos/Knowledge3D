For Designers (Instructions from CHAT) - by harry blazer harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in For Designers (Instructions from CHAT) Sovereignty-Preserving Design Principles for Embodied Systems harry blazer Feb 09, 2026 1 Share Status Normative guidance for engineers, architects, product designers, and system builders working on embodied, autonomous, or semi-autonomous systems intended to operate in human environments. This document defines what designers must and must not build , independent of capability, market demand, or institutional pressure. 1. Purpose and Scope This document exists to prevent a category error that has already occurred once with artificial intelligence: confusing technical capability with legitimate authority . Designers are often the last humans who can meaningfully constrain a system before it becomes operational force . This document establishes the non-negotiable boundaries within which design may proceed. It applies to: robotics engineers AI/ML system designers embedded systems architects autonomy and control engineers product managers with system-level authority safety and compliance engineers 2. Foundational Principle: Designers Do Not Grant Authority Designers do not grant authority. Designers only implement constraints on authority granted elsewhere. No technical role — including engineering excellence, safety intent, or institutional mandate — confers the right to create or extend coercive power. If a system appears to exercise authority, that authority must: originate outside the system, remain accountable to human actors, and be revocable without system cooperation. Any design that embeds authority inside the system violates this principle. 3. Sovereignty Invariants (Non-Negotiable) All embodied systems interacting with humans MUST preserve the following invariants: Human sovereignty is primary Systems exist to serve human purposes, not to impose outcomes. Autonomous coercion is prohibited No system may independently apply force, restraint, pursuit, or compelled compliance to a human being. Authority is not software Authority may not be encoded as internal policy, learned behavior, or optimization objective. Failure must be survivable When systems fail, humans must retain agency, exit and self-defense. If any invariant cannot be preserved, the system must not be built. 4. Boundary Architecture Obligations (High-Level) Designers MUST ensure that any embodied system includes: Separation of decision and actuation authorization The system that decides what to do must not be the system that authorizes physical force. Upstream interlocks Physical actuation must be mediated by a boundary mechanism not under the control of the main intelligence. Safe-state enforcement Ambiguity, sensor conflict, or boundary violation attempts must default to reduced capability, not escalation. Authority immutability at runtime A system’s allowed authority envelope must not be expandable during deployment. Designers may not substitute monitoring, dashboards, or alerts for these obligations. 5. Absolute Prohibition: Backdoors This must be stated without ambiguity. No embodied system may contain backdoors of any kind. This includes, but is not limited to: hidden override commands undocumented access paths latent administrative modes emergency control channels vendor-only access keys government-only access mechanisms “lawful access” pathways not visible to the operator Rationale Backdoors: collapse trust asymmetrically, invite coercive capture, become targets for abuse, and function as pre-authorized sovereignty violations. A system that can be secretly overridden is not constrained — it is conditionally dominated . If emergency intervention is required, it must occur through visible, auditable, non-secret mechanisms that do not expand authority. 6. Prohibition on Silent or Latent Capability Latent coercive capability is coercive capability. Designers MUST NOT: include dormant force capabilities “for future use” expose attachment points for coercive tools ship feature-flagged restraint, pursuit or enforcement functions include APIs that could unlock coercion later rely on “we won’t enable it” assurances If a capability can be activated by: software update, configuration change, hardware attachment, key insertion, …it must be treated as present from inception and governed accordingly. 7. Anti-Patterns: What Does Not Count as Safety Designers are explicitly warned against the following patterns, which do not constitute real safety: post-hoc auditing without actuation interlocks cloud-only kill switches safety logic running in the same trust domain as the agent logs that the system itself curates or can suppress emergency modes that expand authority “human in the loop” claims without veto power dashboards that observe but cannot intervene If the system can ignore safety mechanisms, those mechanisms are not safety — they are theater. 8. Consent and Refusal Awareness Designers must assume that: consent can be revoked, presence does not imply consent, compliance under force is not consent, inability to exit invalidates consent. Systems must be designed such that refusal does not trigger retaliation , degradation of rights, or data penalty. If refusal cannot be honored, the system does not belong in human space. 9. Refusal to Build Is a Valid Design Outcome This document explicitly affirms the following: When a system cannot be made to comply with sovereignty boundaries, the correct response is refusal — not mitigation. Designers are not obligated to: “make it safer” when it is structurally unsafe, “balance risks” when sovereignty is at stake, comply with directives that require embedding coercive capability. Escalation, documentation and refusal are legitimate professional actions. 10. Relationship to the Illuminator Designers should understand the Illuminator correctly: It is a verification system , not a command authority. It proves constraints; it does not grant permission. It witnesses events; it does not decide outcomes. It triggers consequences; it does not issue orders. Designers must not turn the Illuminator into: a centralized control tower, a discretionary override system, a bureaucratic enforcement engine. Doing so recreates the very domination structure this framework exists to prevent. 11. Designer Responsibility Statement Design choices shape reality. Every boundary omitted is a power granted. Every backdoor added is a future abuse enabled. Every latent capability is a silent decision about sovereignty. Designers are not neutral intermediaries. They are stewards of the boundary between tool and domination. Closing This document exists to ensure that engineering excellence does not become an instrument of quiet coercion . If a system cannot coexist with human sovereignty, it is not a technical challenge — it is a moral stop sign. © Harry Blazer. All rights reserved.
