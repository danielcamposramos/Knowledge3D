MORE ON ANIMAL FATS AS A PRIMORDIAL NUTRIENT harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in MORE ON ANIMAL FATS AS A PRIMORDIAL NUTRIENT harry blazer Dec 06, 2025 3 1 1 Share Why Plants Can’t Feed the Brain The Nutrient Illusion of Vegetable-Based Diets — and the Case for Animal and Marine Fats The modern world promotes a strange idea: that humans can thrive on vegetables alone, that plants can supply everything we need for energy, immunity, fertility, and cognition. It is a comforting fantasy in an age obsessed with purity, optimization, and engineered simplicity. But biology isn’t linear and it isn’t ideological. It is coherent, ancient, and unforgiving when its requirements aren’t met. And the biochemical truth is stark. Vegetables contain almost none of the fats required to build, maintain, or power the human organism. Not some, not low levels — none in any meaningful biological sense. Yet this obvious fact has been obscured by decades of marketing, nutritional confusion, and the industrial food system’s desire to replace animal fats with patented seed oils. What has been lost in the noise is the essential role of animal and marine fats in human sovereignty. These fats are not luxuries or “options,” they are the foundational substances that built our brains, supported our fertility, powered our metabolism, and shaped our evolutionary trajectory. Plants decorate the diet. Animal and marine fats define the organism. The Problem with Plant-Based Fat: It Isn’t Fat for Humans Vegetables themselves contain almost no fat at all. Leafy greens, roots, stalks, crucifers, and salads clock in under one percent fat, and that fat is almost entirely irrelevant to human physiology. The mistake arises when people imagine that plant oils are the same as plant foods . They are not. Plant oils are industrial products extracted from seeds through intense heat, chemical solvents, bleaching, and deodorizing. No traditional culture ever consumed these oils in quantity. Even then, the fats in plants fail for one fundamental reason: They do not contain the molecules human biology depends on. Plants provide: No DHA No EPA No arachidonic acid No meaningful saturated long-chain fats No cholesterol No vitamin A in active form No vitamin D3 No vitamin K2 MK-4 They offer precursors, not usable nutrients. Beta carotene is not retinol. ALA is not DHA. Phytosterols are not cholesterol. Humans convert these precursors at miserable rates — often below 1 percent. This is why entire populations can appear well-fed yet suffer from the silent deficiencies modernity normalizes as “aging”, “stress”, or “genetics.” The Human Brain Is an Animal-Fat Creation Sixty percent of the brain is fat, but not just any fat. It is built from saturated animal fat, cholesterol, and DHA from marine life. These molecules: Regulate synaptic integrity Build myelin Support cognition Enable hormonal stability Preserve mitochondrial function Maintain membrane fluidity Support neurodevelopment A plant-based fat profile cannot build, repair, or sustain a human brain. It simply doesn’t contain the materials. Evolution’s verdict is unambiguous. The human brain expanded only when our ancestors gained consistent access to animal fat, marrow, organ meats, and marine fats rich in DHA . No other dietary shift explains our cognitive leap. Fish Fats: The Marine Intelligence Upgrade While land animals provide the fat-soluble vitamins and saturated fats that stabilize the organism, marine animals supply the fats that refine it. Cold-water fish, shellfish, fish roe, and marine mammals contain: DHA — the master brain fat EPA — anti-inflammatory and regulatory Marine phospholipids — superior membrane components Highly bioavailable iodine and selenium Astaxanthin and lipid-bound antioxidants DHA is not just another nutrient. It is a structural requirement for human intelligence. Every neuron in your brain, every photoreceptor in your eye, every synapse that encodes memory depends on DHA. And DHA does not exist in plants. It appears in the animal kingdom alone, with its richest concentration in the sea. To deny this is to deny biology itself. The Sovereign Fat Trinity: Land, Sea, and Organ When humans eat in alignment with nature, three categories of fat create a complete metabolic and cognitive architecture: 1. Land Animal Fats Tallow, suet, butterfat, yolk fat. These deliver vitamins A, D, E, and K2 in usable form, gut-healing butyrate, mitochondrial activators, and the hormonal precursors necessary for fertility and resilience. 2. Marine Fats Fish oils, roe, shellfish lipids. These provide DHA, EPA, iodine, selenium, and the essential compounds for neurodevelopment and cognitive maintenance. 3. Organ Fats and Marrow The densest source of fat-soluble vitamins and enzymatic cofactors on earth. Together they knit the entire organism into a coherent whole. This is the blueprint of nutrient sovereignty — the internal coherence of the living system that no digital or industrial substitution can replicate. Seed Oils: The Digital Fat Impersonators The rise of industrial seed oils has created the illusion that plants contain meaningful fat. But these oils are not food, they are engineered commodities . They destabilize membranes, oxidize easily, disrupt hormones, promote inflammation, and lack the vitamins that animal and marine fats deliver effortlessly. Seed oils are to nutrition what deepfakes are to identity — a mimicry without coherence, a replacement without essence. They belong to the digital paradigm: replace the real with the modifiable, the ancient with the patentable, the coherent with the scalable. Vegetables Are Accessories, Not Architecture Vegetables offer minerals, fiber, antioxidants, and variety. They are useful. They can even be medicinal. But they cannot form the foundation of human nutrition. They cannot build the brain, sustain hormones, support fertility, or maintain immunity. To rely on vegetables for essential fats is to build a temple on sand. To rely on animal and marine fats is to build on bedrock. This is not ideology — it is physiology. And physiology does not negotiate. Nutrient Sovereignty Is Human Sovereignty A population disconnected from animal and marine fats becomes weak, confused, compliant, and dependent. A population nourished by them becomes strong, vital, discerning, and resilient. Strength is not only political or philosophical. It is biological. And biological sovereignty begins with reclaiming the fats that built us. Vegetables are adornments. Seed oils are distortions. Animal and marine fats are the architecture of human life. This is the return to nutrient sovereignty — and the restoration of human coherence in an incoherent age. 3 1 1 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks Bill Niman Dec 6 This is a must read for every vegan.
