Local Incoherence and the Limits of Explanation harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in Local Incoherence and the Limits of Explanation Why Reality Does Not Require Agency to Surprise Us harry blazer Jan 06, 2026 2 Share There are moments in life that do not feel extraordinary when they occur, yet later refuse to reconcile themselves with the rest of reality. Nothing dramatic happens. Consciousness remains continuous. The world does not shimmer or dissolve. And yet, when events are later compared across people, trajectories or timelines, something fails to line up. Time appears to have slipped — not stopped, not reversed, not broken — but failed to coordinate. I have experienced a small number of such moments. They were not mystical in tone. They did not announce themselves with fear or awe. If anything, they felt ordinary — until ordinary explanations failed. That distinction matters. A Boundary Experience: When Time Failed to Coordinate In one instance, I was riding a bike in Maui with a friend, heading toward a beach where we were both going to participate in an activity. I was ahead, reached a clearly defined turnoff from the main road and stopped to wait for her. There was no alternative route. Visibility was unobstructed. I waited for what felt like ten minutes. No one appeared. Assuming she had stopped or fallen behind, I continued on to the beach — only to find her already there. The only way she could have arrived was by passing me at the turn where I had been waiting. She had not seen me. I had not seen her. There was no sensation of lost time on my part. No discontinuity of consciousness. No confusion. And yet, something had clearly failed to line up. The anomaly did not exist in my experience alone . It existed in the irreconcilability of our shared account . Another Instance: Compression Without Discontinuity In a different setting, walking at night in Europe, I approached a street and looked to my right. A car was far enough away that I could judge its speed and distance with confidence. I began to cross — a crossing that would have taken ten seconds at most. Halfway across, I nearly collided with the side of the car and had to jump back. There is no plausible way the car could have traversed that distance in that time. There is no plausible way the driver would not have seen me and slowed. There was no lapse of attention. No blackout. No gap in awareness. Again: continuity internally, incoherence relationally. What These Experiences Are — and Are Not These experiences do not prove anything exotic. They do not announce hidden beings, overseers or interventions. They do not require aliens, gods, simulations or tricksters to make sense. But they also cannot be dismissed as imagination, confusion or error. They point instead to something quieter and more unsettling: Reality can remain globally coherent while failing to coordinate locally. That is not mysticism. It is structure. Local Incoherence as a Physics-Ally Possibility The core claim that follows from these experiences is simple, and deliberately modest: Local temporal/spatial incoherence is “physics-ally” possible without disturbing global coherence or requiring agency of any kind (including simulation, which is itself a form of agency). This does not exclude the possibility of agency — it just removes the necessity for it. “Physics-ally” matters here. It means non-violative . It means nothing needs to break the laws of physics for such events to occur. Modern physics already denies a universal “now.” Synchronization is not guaranteed; it is achieved. Most of the time, it holds well enough that we mistake it for necessity. Occasionally, it does not. When that coordination fails locally, reality does not collapse. It simply becomes non-reconstructible . Why Agency Is So Tempting — and So Often Invoked When coordination fails, humans reach for intention. Across history, people have spoken of gods, angels, spirits and messengers. Modern people also speak of aliens, interdimensionals, simulations and hidden systems. Importantly, modern people still speak of gods, angels and spirits as well. These vocabularies coexist. They overlap. They persist. This persistence tells us something important: The phenomenon precedes the narrative . But narrative persistence does not establish necessity. Agency explanations may be: historically persistent symbolically meaningful psychologically compelling But none of that makes them required . Local incoherence allows anomalous events without invoking intention at all . That is the crucial demotion. Possibility vs. Necessity Nothing in this framework forbids agency. Nothing rules it out. But once agency is no longer required , it loses its default status. That distinction matters. It prevents us from turning every seam in reality into a story about overseers — whether divine, extraterrestrial or computational. It keeps inquiry open instead of closing it with comfort. Why Digital Descriptions of Reality Fail at the Boundary Local incoherence exposes a hard limit on digital representations of reality. Digital systems require: total ordering complete logs reconstructible histories external verification Local incoherence violates all of these. Some events are ontologically real but informationally incomplete . They happen. They matter. They bind the people who experience them. Yet they cannot be fully reconstructed afterward. To a digital system, such events look like errors. To reality, they are simply unclosed (and undisclosed). Human Sovereignty as a Structural Necessity This is where sovereignty becomes unavoidable. If reality itself is not fully synchronizable, then no external system can claim final authority over lived truth . Responsibility cannot be outsourced to logs. Meaning cannot be reduced to records. Ethics cannot be automated. Only a sovereign human being can: act under uncertainty bear responsibility without total explanation live truthfully when coherence is lived rather than recorded (explained further in footnote*) This is not sentimentality. It is structural. Holding Reality Open Local incoherence teaches restraint. It teaches us not to rush toward stories that close the question too quickly — whether those stories are theological, technological or speculative. It also teaches us not to dismiss experiences simply because they resist tidy explanation. Reality does not promise perfect coordination. It promises coherence enough to live. That gap — between coherence and completeness — is not a flaw. It is the space in which freedom, responsibility and sovereignty exist at all. Closing Thought The universe does not need an agent in order to surprise us. And the absence of necessity is not the absence of meaning. Sometimes, reality simply reminds us that it is larger than our explanations — and that no system, however powerful, gets the final word. *”live truthfully when coherence is lived rather than recorded” means this: 1. Coherence can be enacted, not captured There are forms of coherence that exist only in action , judgment, and lived alignment — not in data, logs, metrics or formal proofs. A person can be coherent in how they live , even if that coherence cannot be fully articulated, audited, or reconstructed after the fact. Examples: A moral stance held consistently across changing circumstances without being reducible to a rule Integrity expressed through choice, not explanation Wisdom that shows itself in timing, restraint, or refusal, not in argument This coherence is real , but it is not always legible. 2. Truth does not require full external verification “Recorded” coherence is the kind demanded by systems: documentation transparency dashboards explainability requirements compliance artifacts audit trails These are representations of coherence, not coherence itself. The phrase asserts that a sovereign human can live truthfully without needing their inner consistency to be fully externalized , digitized or made machine-legible. In other words: Truth can be embodied without being serialized ( To convert an object into a sequence of bytes that can later be converted back into an object with equivalent properties.) 3. Lived coherence tolerates ambiguity Recorded coherence demands: completeness consistency across time contradiction-free narratives Lived coherence tolerates: silence paradox partial articulation evolving understanding A sovereign person may act rightly before they can explain why — or even when explanation would distort the truth of the act. This is not irrationality. It is pre-formal rationality — the kind that precedes models. 4.
