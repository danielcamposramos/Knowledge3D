The question was no longer how to protect the public from danger. It was how to protect essential systems from accountability . Medicine and software would become the proving grounds. The Migration of the Nuclear Template Once law accepted the idea that certain activities were too important to fail , it became possible to reframe liability not as justice, but as a threat to continuity . The logic was simple: If accountability undermines progress, accountability must yield. This is the logic that carried nuclear immunity into medicine. VICP: When Harm Became Administrative The National Vaccine Injury Compensation Program (VICP), created by the National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act of 1986, was publicly framed as a compassionate alternative to adversarial litigation. In practice, it did something far more consequential: Removed vaccine injury claims from civil courts Replaced jury trials with administrative adjudication Shifted evidentiary burdens Narrowed discovery Capped and structured compensation Shielded manufacturers from direct liability This was not deregulation . It was jurisdictional displacement . Just as nuclear risk was moved from private insurance markets to statute, medical injury was moved from common law into a specialized administrative silo. PREP Act: Emergency as a Permanent Condition The PREP Act completed the migration. Under the PREP Act: Immunity is broad Accountability is narrow Remedies are limited Emergency declarations unlock protection Duration is elastic The nuclear logic is unmistakable: When the stakes are represented to be high enough, consent and recourse become luxuries. The Crucial Shift: Harm Reframed as Systemic Risk This is the pivot point. In both nuclear and pharmaceutical regimes: Harm to individuals is acknowledged But liability is framed as a threat to the system Therefore, justice is subordinated to continuity The injured party becomes an unfortunate externality of progress. This is where ethics quietly invert . The Cultural Translation: IT Discovers the Same Trick Now we arrive at a parallel that most often goes unmentioned. The IT industry did not need statutory immunity. It discovered something even more powerful: De facto immunity through essentiality. Microsoft and the Abandonment of the Service Ethic By the late 1980s and 1990s, Microsoft had achieved something unprecedented. Its products — Word, Excel, Windows — were no longer tools. They were infrastructure . And once infrastructure status is achieved, a radical inversion becomes possible: Bugs become “known issues” Crashes become “unavoidable” Security failures become “patch cycles” Downtime becomes “maintenance” Data loss becomes “terms and conditions” Prototyping is subsidized by the customer In any non-IT industry, this would have been fatal. But Microsoft proved a new law of power: If a product is perceived as essential, the producer no longer has to serve the customer. The customer must adapt to the product. Inconvenience or worse is the price of entry. This mirrors Price–Anderson perfectly. From Legal Immunity to Cultural Immunity The IT industry internalized the lesson without Congressional intervention: Externalize operational risk Shift responsibility to users Replace accountability with documentation Substitute End User License Agreements for duty of care Where nuclear immunity was statutory, IT immunity was contractual . Where pharma immunity was administrative, IT immunity was normative . The result is the same: Risk is externalized. Power persists. The Foundational Cultural Shift This was not just a business model change. It was a civilizational shift . Once: Service was prerequisite to survival Reliability was expected Failure carried reputational consequences After IT: Failure is normalized Updates replace repair Users absorb instability Disregard for the customer becomes structural The customer-service ethic did not disappear accidentally. It became extraneous in systems accepted as essential . Why This Matters for AI AI inherits every advantage of nuclear, pharma and IT: Complexity beyond lay understanding Black-box behavior Scale effects Emergency framing Essentiality narratives Rapid dependency creation The groundwork has already been laid. The argument will not be: “AI should be accountable.” It will be: “AI is too important to constrain.” And the legal, contractual and cultural machinery is already waiting. Continuity of the Pattern Across domains, the same sequence appears: Technology becomes essential Risk becomes uninsurable Accountability threatens continuity Law or contract intervenes Harm is administratively managed Culture adapts to power, not the reverse This is not corruption. It is systemic self-aggrandizement . Nuclear power taught the state how to socialize catastrophic risk. Medicine taught it how to disguise immunity as compassion. Software taught society how to accept dysfunction as normal — provided the system is deemed essential. 1 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks Top Latest Discussions No posts Ready for more?
