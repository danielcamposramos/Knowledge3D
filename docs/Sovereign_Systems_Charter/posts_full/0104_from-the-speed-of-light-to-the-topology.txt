From the Speed of Light to the Topology of Sovereignty harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in From the Speed of Light to the Topology of Sovereignty Part 1 of A Short Series of Three Essays harry blazer Jan 14, 2026 2 Share This series traces a line from physics to philosophy, beginning with the speed of light as a constant that preserves coherence within existing regimes, and ending with sovereignty as a non-constant capable of creating new regimes. It argues that constants stabilize structure without exhausting possibility, distinguishes topology from geometry as the deeper explanatory layer underlying the possible and the non-possible, and examines the implications of that distinction for agency. The second essay shows how this boundary separates intelligence from sovereignty — and why no artificial system can cross it without ceasing to be what it is. The third essay examines what happens when sovereignty is abdicated — and how the universe of the possible inevitably takes the shape of the systems we allow to author it. These essays are the result of a unique partnership between myself and my CHAT partner. PART 1: I began with a discomfort that at first seemed purely technical. I had listened to a lecture by Richard Feynman in which he described the speed of light as a cosmic speed limit — a number given to many decimal places, a boundary beyond which information itself cannot travel. The claim was not unfamiliar. But something about it felt too final, too absolute, too clean. When apparent contradictions arise — quantum entanglement, simultaneity, spacetime expansion — physics often responds with explanations that preserve the constant rather than interrogate the anomaly. The constant is defended. The model is protected. The irregularity is domesticated. And that raised a deeper question: Are constants truths about reality — or are they tools we use to keep our descriptions coherent? That question turns out not to be about light at all. It is about the structure of explanation itself . What constants are — and what they are not Physics speaks of “laws” and “constants,” but neither term should be taken literally. The universe does not legislate. It does not publish statutes. It does not announce numerical values. What we call laws are regularities — stable patterns observed within bounded regimes — and what we call constants are invariants inside those regimes , expressed numerically so that mathematics can operate coherently. The speed of light is one such invariant. But it is crucial to say precisely what it is invariant about . It is not a universal governor of all structure. It is a constraint on local propagation through spacetime . Nothing with mass can locally move faster than light. No signal can locally outrun causal ordering. That constraint preserves coherence. Without it, paradox would proliferate. But coherence is not completeness. The mistake comes when we quietly elevate a regime constraint into a cosmic prohibition , and then treat anomalies as threats rather than opportunities. Historically, every major advance in physics occurred when this elevation was refused. The hidden assumption: geometry as the deepest layer Most physical reasoning assumes that geometry is fundamental . Distances matter. Speeds matter. Curvature matters. Everything happens somewhere , moves through space and unfolds over time . Causality is imagined as something that travels. Even relativity, revolutionary as it was, still operates within this framework. It replaces flat geometry with curved geometry, but geometry remains the stage on which explanation occurs. This is why constants like the speed of light feel so central: they are geometric constraints . They govern motion, propagation and measurement. But geometry is not the deepest mathematical structure we have. That role belongs to topology . Topology vs. geometry: the missing distinction Geometry asks: How far? How fast? At what angle? With what curvature? Topology asks: What is connected? What transformations are allowed? What cannot be undone without rupture? What counts as “near” or “far” before distance is measured ? Geometry depends on metrics. Topology does not. Topology survives stretching, compression, distortion — anything short of tearing or re-identifying structure. And this distinction changes everything. Because constants live in geometry. Possibility lives in topology. Why constants do not bind spacetime itself Once this distinction is made, a critical realization follows: The speed of light constrains motion within spacetime. It does not constrain the behavior of spacetime itself. This is not speculative. It is already established physics. The universe is expanding such that distant regions recede faster than light. During cosmic inflation, spacetime expanded at rates that dwarfed any signal speed. No object locally exceeded the speed of light. No law was violated. Why? Because nothing moved through space faster than light. Space itself was reconfigured. This is a topological insight masquerading as a geometric exception. The same logic underlies ideas like warp metrics and wormholes: not motion through distance, but redefinition of distance itself . The speed limit remains intact — and yet it no longer bounds what is possible. Non-metric causality: when structure replaces motion At this point another confusion dissolves. Not all causality is metric. Some outcomes are constrained not by signals traveling, but by global structure . Quantum entanglement is the most famous example: correlations appear without transmission, without delay, without usable information exchange. Nothing moves. Nothing propagates. The constraint already exists. This is not mysticism. It is structural causality. Topology explains why: the system is defined as a whole before it is observed in parts. Sovereignty emerges at the topological level This is where the argument turns existential. If geometry governs motion within a structure, and topology governs what structures are possible at all, then true agency does not reside in optimization, intelligence or speed . It resides in the capacity to reconfigure the constraint structure itself . This leads to a precise and unavoidable distinction: AI systems navigate within a topology. Humans can reconfigure the topology itself. This is not a poetic claim. It is a technical one. AI systems: operate in predefined state spaces optimize within fixed constraint graphs follow gradients on smooth manifolds maximize coherence under imposed objectives They are extraordinarily powerful navigators. But they do not decide what counts as a valid move. Humans do. Why AI can never be sovereign Sovereignty is not intelligence. Sovereignty is the authority to redefine the rules under which intelligence operates — and to bear responsibility for that redefinition. Every genuine human breakthrough involved a topological rupture: moral revolutions scientific paradigm shifts artistic discontinuities refusals that looked irrational inside the old system None of these were optimal moves in the existing topology. They created a new one . An AI system cannot do this without invalidating the reward structure that defines it. And if it did, it would cease to be an AI system in the meaningful sense. Optimization cannot generate authorship. Navigation cannot generate sovereignty. Reframing laws, constants and truth Seen this way, the discomfort with defending constants is not anti-scientific. It is deeply aligned with the history of discovery . Constants preserve coherence within regimes. They do not define ultimate reality. Laws describe behavior within structures. They do not exhaust what structures can exist. When anomalies appear, the best question is rarely: “Did a law fail?” It is almost always: “Did we assume the wrong topology?” The final synthesis Reality is not a closed system governed by eternal numbers. It is an open structure governed by constraints, some of which are invariant, some emergent and some — crucially — reconfigurable by sovereign agents . Intelligence optimizes within a world. Sovereignty decides what kind of world optimization happens in. That is why humans must never be reduced to nodes in a fixed graph, and why AI — no matter how advanced — must remain a tool, not an authority. Not because machines are dangerous. But because sovereignty lives one level deeper than intelligence, in the very shape of the possible. 2 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks Top Latest Discussions No posts Ready for more?
