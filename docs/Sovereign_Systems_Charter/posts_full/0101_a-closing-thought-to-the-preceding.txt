A Closing Thought To The Preceding Series: How Constraints Are Categorically Different Than Determinism harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in A Closing Thought To The Preceding Series: How Constraints Are Categorically Different Than Determinism Why structure does not dictate outcome harry blazer Jan 14, 2026 2 Share A common objection surfaces whenever constraints are discussed seriously: Isn’t this just determinism in disguise? If reality is constrained, if structures shape what is possible, then aren’t outcomes effectively preordained? The answer is no — and not by degree, but by category . Confusing constraints with determinism is one of the most consequential conceptual errors of modern thought. It leads directly to fatalism, moral abdication, a reconfiguring of reality and the false belief that sovereignty is an illusion. It is also wrong. Determinism closes the future. Constraints do not. Determinism asserts that: given a complete description of initial conditions and a complete set of governing laws the future is fully entailed In a deterministic universe, novelty is only apparent. Freedom is only ignorance. Choice is a narrative overlay on inevitability. Constraints operate differently. A constraint: limits what cannot happen without specifying what must happen This distinction is decisive. Constraints shape a space of possibilities . They do not select a path within it. A simple analogy Consider the rules of chess. The rules are rigid constraints. They strictly prohibit certain moves. They define what counts as legal play. But they do not determine: what game will be played what strategies will emerge what sacrifices will be made what creative forms will appear Chess is not deterministic in practice, not because it lacks rules, but because rules create space for authorship rather than replacing it. Determinism would require that the game be fully entailed from the opening position. It is not. Constraints enable emergence. Determinism eliminates it. Why constraints are necessary for freedom This is counterintuitive but essential: Freedom does not exist in the absence of constraints. It exists because of them. Without constraints: nothing is meaningful no action excludes another no choice carries weight no commitment can bind A promise only matters because it constrains future action. A moral principle only exists because it forbids certain advantages. Constraints create stakes . Stakes create meaning. Determinism removes stakes by removing authorship. The error of treating constraints as hidden laws Modern systems — especially computational ones — tend to treat constraints as: rules to be enforced boundaries to be optimized against parameters to be satisfied From inside such systems, constraint and determination look indistinguishable. But this is a limitation of the system’s mode of operation , not of reality. A system that can only optimize will always mistake: constraint for control structure for destiny coherence for closure This is why AI systems are so easily framed as “inevitable.” They are not inevitable. They are confined . Constraints allow refusal. Determinism does not. Here is the sharpest dividing line. In a constrained but non-deterministic world, a sovereign being can say: “I could do this — but I will not.” That sentence is unintelligible in a deterministic universe. There, “could” collapses into “would,” and refusal becomes an illusion. But in a constrained world: refusal is real sacrifice is meaningful responsibility is unavoidable Constraints make refusal possible. Determinism makes it incoherent. Why this matters for sovereignty Sovereignty depends on three things: real alternatives meaningful exclusion responsibility for choice Constraints preserve all three. Determinism preserves none. This is why sovereignty is not threatened by acknowledging structure — it is threatened by collapsing structure into inevitability. And this is why systems that operate deterministically (or approximate it through optimization) can never be sovereign, no matter how intelligent they become. They can traverse a space. They cannot author within it. The final distinction Here is the line that should not be crossed: Determinism says the future is already written. Constraints say the future must be written — by someone. That “someone” is the sovereign agent. Remove that agent, and the future collapses into optimization.
