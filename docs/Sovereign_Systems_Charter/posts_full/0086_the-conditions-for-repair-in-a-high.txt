The Conditions for Repair: In a High-Stress, Low-Coherence Society harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in The Conditions for Repair: In a High-Stress, Low-Coherence Society A Followup On The Last Essay harry blazer Jan 20, 2026 Share If families are fracturing under modern conditions, the instinctive response is to ask what individuals should do differently: communicate better, choose more carefully, work harder, adapt faster. These prescriptions are not wrong, but they are incomplete. They assume that repair is primarily a matter of effort. It is not. Repair is a function of conditions . And in a high-stress, low-coherence society, many of the conditions required for relational endurance have quietly disappeared. Before asking people to carry more, we need to ask what makes carrying possible. Repair requires stress reduction, not stress denial The first condition is brutally simple and widely resisted: chronic stress must be reduced , not reframed. No amount of insight, therapy or moral exhortation can compensate for unrelenting pressure. When stress becomes ambient and permanent, it overwhelms even well-functioning relationships. The nervous system does not negotiate with ideology. Repair begins when people can experience periods of genuine relief—predictability, rest and the absence of constant threat signaling. Without this, every interaction is filtered through exhaustion. Conflict becomes more frequent not because people care less, but because they have less margin. Margin is not a luxury. It is a prerequisite. Repair requires temporal depth Endurance depends on the ability to situate the present within a longer arc. In a culture dominated by immediacy—real-time metrics, instant feedback, perpetual comparison—relationships are evaluated moment by moment rather than over seasons. Repair requires temporal depth : the shared understanding that difficulty can exist without defining the whole, that strain does not exhaust meaning, and that continuity itself has value independent of optimization. When time horizons collapse, everything feels final. Under those conditions, exit appears rational because the future no longer provides interpretive relief. Repair requires shared reality Low coherence is not merely disagreement; it is the absence of a common frame within which disagreement can occur. When partners no longer share assumptions about meaning, obligation, identity or truth, conflict cannot resolve—it can only escalate or terminate. Repair requires some restored zone of shared reality , even if narrow: agreement on what matters agreement on what is real agreement on what is non-negotiable This does not require total alignment. It requires a stable reference point. Without it, every issue becomes existential. Repair requires de-privatization of strain One of the most destructive features of modern life is the privatization of struggle. Families are treated as self-contained units responsible for absorbing forces generated far beyond their control. Repair requires strain to be made visible and shared again: through community through kinship through truthful language through mutual recognition When stress is named collectively, shame diminishes. When shame diminishes, cooperation becomes possible. Isolation is not merely lonely—it is destabilizing. No relationship repairs in a vacuum. Repair requires moral permission to endure Endurance has been culturally reframed as weakness, fear or lack of self-respect. This has consequences. When people lack moral permission to stay through difficulty, they interpret perseverance as self-betrayal. Repair requires restoring the legitimacy of endurance—not as blind loyalty or self-erasure, but as a conscious, dignified choice made in recognition of long-term meaning. Endurance is not denial. It is orientation. Repair requires limits on optimization Optimization is corrosive to relationships. When every aspect of life is evaluated in terms of efficiency, satisfaction or return, commitment becomes fragile by design. Repair requires domains that are exempt from optimization logic . Relationships must be allowed to be inefficient, asymmetric and at times unsatisfying without being considered failures. Not everything valuable improves under constant measurement. Repair requires agency without self-blame Finally, repair requires a delicate balance: individuals must retain agency without being crushed by false responsibility. This means: acknowledging structural constraints without surrendering choice exercising accountability without internalizing systemic failure acting deliberately without believing that everything rests on personal virtue Agency grounded in clarity is stabilizing. Agency grounded in guilt is not. A closing realism Repair, under current conditions, will not be universal. Some fractures are too advanced. Some environments are too hostile. Some costs are too high. But clarity matters even when outcomes vary. When people understand that repair depends on conditions—not just willpower—they stop mistaking exhaustion for incompatibility, strain for failure and difficulty for error. That alone changes how choices are made. In a high-stress, low-coherence society, repair is not about heroics. It is about rebuilding the ground on which endurance can stand. Without that ground, asking people to “try harder” is not advice. It is another stressor.
