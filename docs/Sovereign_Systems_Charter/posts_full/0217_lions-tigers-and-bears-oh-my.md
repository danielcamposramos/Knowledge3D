## Summary
Our digestive system says: We digest starch extremely well (amylase copy number explosion) Our colon can ferment fiber into short-chain fatty acids Our stomach acidity is less extreme Our small intestine is longer We can tolerate some plant antinutrients, but not all Our liver cannot make certain vitamins that cats can (e.g., vitamin A preformed) Humans evolved to fill the niche of: “eat whatever is available, whenever it’s available.” This flexibility helped us survive climate cycles, seasonal famine, and migration. Vegan diets cannot meet human biological requirements without supplements Nutrients missing entirely from vegan diets: Vitamin B12 (zero in plants; deficiency causes irreversible neurological damage) Preformed DHA and EPA (brain-essential omega-3s; plant ALA converts poorly) Vitamin K2 (MK-4) (crucial for bone and arterial health) Creatine (major brain and muscle energy buffer) Taurine (cardiac, retinal, and metabolic roles) Carnosine (anti-glycation, muscle and CNS functioning) Heme iron (plants contain non-heme iron, poorly absorbed) Preformed vitamin A (retinol) (plants have carotenoids, poorly converted) Choline (exists in plants but far lower, poor bioavailability) Glycine / collagen sources No amount of spinach or broccoli replaces these. PART III — SUMMARY OF ALL KEY POINTS Why vegan/vegetarian diets stress humans: Missing essential nutrients Poor protein quality Anti-nutrient interference Hormonal disruption Greater dependency on glucose metabolism Lack of key brain- and immune-critical molecules Why animal-based diets fit human physiology: Complete amino acid profiles Fat-soluble vitamins Bioavailable minerals DHA/EPA, creatine, choline Stable glucose & insulin dynamics Alignment with ancestral eating patterns How humans compare to mountain lions: The big picture: Humans sit halfway between: pure carnivores (like lions) and plant-adapted primates (like gorillas) But our brain, gut, enzymes, and biochemistry clearly show: We are evolved for nutrient-dense animal foods, with plants as supplemental survival foods, not primary fuels.
