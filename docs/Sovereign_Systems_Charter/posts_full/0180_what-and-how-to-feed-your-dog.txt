Manyt buy the “organic” kibble and the expensive canned foods and assumed that because the label sounded clean, what was inside must be healthy. But here’s the truth: Even the best kibble is still an ultra-processed industrial product that has almost nothing to do with what a dog is biologically designed to eat. Dogs thrive on the same fundamental principle humans do: Real food. Minimal processing. Species-appropriate nutrition. Below is the simple, sovereign, common-sense guide to feeding your dog in a way that aligns with nature instead of corporate marketing. Fresh Beats Organic When the Alternative Is Processed People get hung up on whether meat is organic, and while I prefer organic any day of the week, here is the key insight: A bowl of fresh, whole, non-organic beef or pork is still far healthier than any organic kibble or organic canned food. Kibble is heated, extruded, oxidized, and rebuilt with synthetic vitamins after the real ones are destroyed. The fats go rancid. The carbs dominate. The proteins are biologically distorted. Fresh meat — even conventional meat — behaves like food in a dog’s body. Kibble behaves like a biologically foreign substance. That’s the hierarchy, and it isn’t even close. Use a Rotation of Real Meats: Raw Beef, Lightly Cooked Pork, Raw Chicken Here’s the foundational mix that works beautifully and mimics natural prey patterns: Beef (raw): Safe, nutrient dense, low parasite risk Pork (cooked to pink): Highly digestible and energy rich Chicken (raw with bone): Bones are soft and safe only when raw — never cooked unless pressure-cooked until mush Dogs are built to crush raw poultry bones. Raw drumsticks, thighs, and legs are exactly the right size for natural chewing and calcium intake. You can get all of this from Costco or a grocery store for less than premium “pet food,” and your dog will be getting actual nutrition instead of synthetic approximations. Add Organs: The Multivitamins of Nature Meat alone is not enough. Predators in the wild go for the organs first because that’s where the nutrients are. I use organic organ meats when possible. The rotation can include: liver (in small amounts, about 1–2 percent of the diet). You don’t want to overdue the vitamin A heart kidney spleen Organs provide vitamins, minerals, taurine, copper and other critical nutrients that no kibble can replicate without synthetic additives. Bones Are Not Optional — When Raw, They’re Essential Raw bones provide: calcium phosphorus collagen dental cleaning gut health mental enrichment Raw chicken bones are safe. Cooked bones are not — unless pressure-cooked until soft enough to crumble between your fingers. If the dog is eating raw chicken legs, thighs or wings, they’re getting exactly what nature intended. The Two Most Important Supplements: Fish and Iodine Modern meats are low in omega-3s and iodine because of industrial feeding practices. You can correct both with simple additions: Sardines or mackerel once or twice a week A pinch of kelp powder a few times a week, or use egg yolks and seafood to balance iodine naturally These small additions transform coat quality, reduce inflammation, and support thyroid health. 6. What You Don’t Need Once you’re feeding real food, you can forget: synthetic multivitamins “formulated” dog chow carbohydrate fillers seed oils preservatives industrial pet-food marketing entirely Your dog doesn’t need grains, peas, lentils, “pea protein,” soy, or powdered fractions of anything. They need prey-model nutrition, not factory extrusion. We live in a world where the food system for humans and animals alike has been captured by the same forces — industrialization, ultra-processing, shelf-life optimization and profit over biology. Feeding your dog real food is not just a health choice. It is an act of sovereignty. It is the recognition that living systems deserve living nourishment, not a dehydrated, extruded simulacrum. I have been using this approach for years — raw beef, raw chicken with bone, lightly cooked pork, organic organs, natural bones and simple supplements. The result: cleaner teeth better energy calmer nervous system healthier coat smaller, firmer stools better digestion more vitality It’s remarkable what happens when you stop fighting biology and start feeding it. Here is the entire guide in one shot: Feed fresh meat, not processed kibble Use raw beef, raw chicken with bone, and lightly cooked pork Add 5–10 percent organ meat Give raw bones regularly Add sardines and a little iodine Avoid seed oils and carb fillers If you do this, your dog will be eating better than 99 percent of dogs in America — and you’ll be doing it for a similar or lower cost than “premium” processed pet food. This is how you feed a dog in alignment with nature. Not as a consumer, but as a caretaker. Not through marketing, but through knowledge. You honor the life in them by giving them food that contains life. That’s sovereignty — even in something as simple as a dog bowl. 1 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks Top Latest Discussions No posts Ready for more?
