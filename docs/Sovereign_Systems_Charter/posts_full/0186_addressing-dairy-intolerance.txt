Carmen Lilly MY ANSWER: Why Some People Still React Poorly to Even Good Dairy Carmen’s point is real: some people, even after detoxification and gut repair, remain sensitive. This does not invalidate dairy as a sovereign food. It reveals something different: Past antibiotic exposure Childhood formula feeding Industrial seed oils altering gut permeability Glyphosate exposure disrupting tight junctions Heavy metals Immune hyperactivation from chronic stress Long-term microbiome loss When the gut has been broken repeatedly, even ancestral foods can trigger reactions. But this is a downstream effect of industrial disruption, not evidence that dairy is biologically inappropriate. The mistake is treating downstream symptoms as upstream causes. Dairy, Immunity, and Inflammation: Sorting Causes from Consequences Inflammation triggered by dairy usually reflects: impaired barrier integrity insufficient lactase expression dysbiosis* casein sensitivity (not lactose) A1 beta-casein inflammatory peptides systemic burden from modern life When people switch to: raw milk A2 milk goat or sheep milk fermented dairy pasture-raised dairy many tolerate it perfectly. This shows the problem is in the processing not the food, and in the condition of the person not the nature of “real” dairy. * Dysbiosis is a disrupted gut ecosystem that allows harmful microbes to dominate and creates inflammation, food intolerances and metabolic instability. The Sovereignty Principle: Ancestral Foods Are Not the Enemy “There is no way the body was designed to be allergic to ancestral foods done right.” When people react to: meat eggs dairy fermented foods the issue is almost always: prior damage industrial interference disrupted signaling chronic inflammation dysbiotic microbiome The solution is restoration, not elimination. A sovereign organism restores its relationship with real foods. A captured organism restricts itself endlessly. The Distinction Between Symptom and Substance Carmen’s experience is valid. Many people are inflamed by dairy. But she is treating the downstream manifestation of upstream industrial harm. The correct framing is: People do not react to dairy. They react to industrialization, dysbiosis, toxic load and immune dysregulation — and dairy exposes what is already broken. Just as someone with severe gut damage may react to meat, eggs or sauerkraut, the reaction does not make those foods non-human. It reveals the depth of biological injury. Her caution is genuine but incomplete. The problem is not “milk.” The problem is: processed milk processed humans processed ecosystems When the body is restored, the relationship with ancestral foods often returns. When someone reacts negatively to “real” (ancestral) food despite previous efforts to repair a persons damaged body, the probability is that there are still injuries to be repaired. That is what the intolerance is revealing. And the reality is that sometimes damage can be so extensive and chronic, that it can become a long-term disability. Dairy Is a Sovereign Food When It Remains a Real Food The idea that “adults shouldn’t consume dairy” arises from: industrial distortion of milk industrial distortion of human biology ideological hostility toward animal agriculture the general collapse of microbial and metabolic resilience confusion between ancestral foods and their degraded industrial analogs Real dairy — raw, grass-fed, enzyme-active, microbially alive — is one of the most profound nutritional gifts humans have ever accessed. Industrial dairy is not dairy. Industrial reactions are not evolutionary truths. Industrial fragility is not destiny. A sovereign human reclaims ancestral foods, restores biological resilience and refuses to let industrial conditions redefine human nature. 3 Share Previous Next Discussion about this post Comments Restacks Top Latest Discussions No posts Ready for more?
