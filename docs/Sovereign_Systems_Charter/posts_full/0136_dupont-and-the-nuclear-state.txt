DuPont and the Nuclear State - by harry blazer harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in DuPont and the Nuclear State Chemistry, War and Federally Immunized Risk harry blazer Jan 04, 2026 2 Share Core thesis DuPont was not a peripheral contractor in the nuclear age. It was one of the principal industrial architects of the American nuclear system — military first, commercial second — and it would not have participated without extraordinary liability protections granted by the federal government. This is not conjecture. It is openly acknowledged in policy and statute. 1. DuPont and the Birth of the Nuclear Weapons Complex Manhattan Project DuPont was selected as the prime industrial contractor for plutonium production during the Manhattan Project. Why DuPont? Not because it was “just a contractor,” but because: It had unmatched experience in large-scale hazardous chemistry It knew how to manage explosive, toxic and radioactive processes It could build and operate entire industrial cities under secrecy Hanford Site DuPont designed, built and initially operated the plutonium production reactors at Hanford Site . Key facts: First full-scale plutonium production reactors in history Produced plutonium for Nagasaki Generated massive radioactive waste still unmanaged today DuPont later withdrew from direct operation, but the design decisions were irreversible . This is a recurring theme: DuPont builds the system, then exits before long-term consequences surface. 2. Savannah River and the Permanent Nuclear Arsenal DuPont also designed and operated the Savannah River Site , another major plutonium and tritium production complex. Savannah River: Produced materials for thermonuclear weapons Ran for decades Created enduring environmental contamination Again: DuPont designed the chemical separations DuPont trained successor operators DuPont left behind a permanent radioactive legacy 3. The Absolute Requirement: Liability Immunity Here is the critical pivot point . DuPont explicitly refused to participate in nuclear work unless it was fully shielded from liability . This demand directly led to the Price-Anderson Act (1957). What Price–Anderson does Caps industry liability in nuclear accidents Transfers catastrophic risk to: Taxpayers The public Future generations Applies to both: Military nuclear activities Commercial nuclear power Without Price–Anderson: No private insurer would underwrite nuclear risk No corporation would accept the exposure The civilian nuclear industry would not exist This is not debated. It is openly admitted. Nuclear power is not a free market industry. It is a federally subsidized, legally immunized risk regime . 4. DuPont’s Dual-Use Mastery: Military → Commercial Once nuclear chemistry and reactor systems were established under military secrecy, they transitioned into civilian power. This pattern mirrors earlier DuPont behavior: Explosives → mining and construction Chemical weapons → industrial chemistry Nuclear weapons → civilian reactors But nuclear was different: Failure modes were existential Cleanup timelines were geological Liability exposure was infinite Hence: statutory immunity was not optional. 5. Nuclear Feedstocks and Chemical Separation DuPont’s unique value was not reactor physics. It was: Chemical separation of plutonium and uranium Solvent extraction Waste stream handling Materials resistant to radiation and corrosion In other words: The bomb is physics. The system is chemistry. That distinction is crucial. 6. Long-Term Consequences: Socialized Harm, Privatized Expertise Today: Hanford remains one of the most contaminated sites on Earth Savannah River requires perpetual management Cleanup costs are borne by the public Original designers are insulated This establishes a template that later appears elsewhere: Environmental toxins Persistent chemicals Pharmaceutical harms Industrial pollution Design → profit → exit → immunity → public liability 7. Why This Segment Is So Fruitful Because here, the pattern is explicit : A private corporation Engaged in ultra-hazardous activity On behalf of national security With full foreknowledge of catastrophic risk Demanded and received federal legal immunity Creating a precedent later reused across industries This is not ideology. It is institutional fact . 8. Synthesis Statement DuPont did not merely help build the nuclear age; it helped invent the legal doctrine that catastrophic industrial risk should be borne by the public while expertise and control remain private. That doctrine did not stay in nuclear power. It metastasized. 9. Why This Matters to the War We Are Fighting Now The nuclear model becomes the template for: Vaccines with liability shields Environmental contamination without redress Digital systems without accountability AI systems without recourse Risk without consent.
