The Film Score - by harry blazer - harry’s Substack harry’s Substack Subscribe Sign in The Film Score Completion by Resonance harry blazer Jan 18, 2026 1 Share There is a difference between something that works and something that completes . You can have a strong script, excellent acting and beautiful cinematography — and still feel that something hasn’t fully landed. Everything makes sense. Nothing is wrong. Yet the experience remains partially assembled rather than complete. Then the music enters — and suddenly magic. This is not because the music tells us what to feel. It is because the music magnifies the resonance already embedded in the experience . Coherence As Resonance, Not Just Logic Coherence is not merely that everything makes sense. Coherence arises when complementary forms of resonance align — sight, sound, narrative, performance and inner emotional state. Each element creates its own experience, and each contributes to the overall emotional gestalt and impact. Nothing duplicates. Nothing competes. Each component reinforces the same emotional reality from a different direction. When that happens, the experience stops being assembled and starts being lived . The Emotion Is Already There In a great film, emotion exists before a single note is played. The script initiates it. The actors embody it. The images frame it By the time the music arrives, the emotional field is already charged. A great composer does not impose emotion onto that field. They magnify it. Music Completes What Is Emerging Great film music does not add something foreign. It completes . Remove the music and the scene still functions — but it feels incomplete. The emotional arc rises but it never quite fills. Through choices of key, instrumentation, melodic theme and supporting harmony, the composer reaffirms what the script and actors are already putting forth. The music locks onto the same frequency and raises the gain. Not louder per se. Truer. More impactful. It raises the gain until the emotion fills and fulfills . Resonance Is Felt in the Body Resonance is not metaphorical. When sight, sound and story vibrate together: breath deepens the chest opens the nervous system settles chills, warmth or tears emerge without effort That tingling sensation people struggle to describe is not sentimentality. It is the body recognizing that all is in phase . The emotion has been confirmed from multiple directions at once. Vindication, Not Manipulation This is why the listener does not feel pushed. They feel fulfilled . The music does not instruct the audience what to feel. It quietly confirms it. Yes — that feeling you’re having is real. Yes — it matters. We will help complete it. That sensation is vindication, not persuasion. A Concrete Illustration Imagine the movie The Natural exactly as it is — same story, same performances, same images — but without the score. The narrative would remain coherent. The acting would remain strong. But the emotional experience would stall short of fulfillment. The music does not invent the triumph, the longing or the redemption. Those are already present. It magnifies the resonance embedded in the total experience and completes it. What This Kind of Scoring Requires This kind of scoring requires sincerity . It requires release . It requires empathy . And finally, it requires alignment . Sincerity allows the composer to be emotionally present. Release allows them to let go of control and enhance the flow. Empathy allows them to feel the emotional reality. Alignment – the act of bringing those emotional capacities into resonance with what the story, performances and images are expressing. Great film music does not decorate an experience. It does not compete with story or performance. It does not shout. It listens — and then resonates . And when it does, the total experience emerges. Great music completes an experience by magnifying the resonance already embedded within it, until the emotion fills and fulfills. That’s not theory.
