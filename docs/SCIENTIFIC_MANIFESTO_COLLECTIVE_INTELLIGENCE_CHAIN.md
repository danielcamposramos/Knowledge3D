## 1. Premise: SGI as Collective Intelligence, Not a Single Brain

The last decade has been consumed by arguments about *AGI* and *superintelligence*—as if the end state of AI were a single, towering mind built by ever‑larger matrices of weights. This is the “tower of Babel” story told in GPU clusters, driven by the belief that scaling alone will deliver understanding and control.

History tells a different story:

- The real breakthroughs have always come from **collective intelligence**: scientists exchanging letters, open‑source communities shipping kernels, activists sharing knowledge across borders.  
- The web itself, which Aaron Swartz defended, was built as a **network of intentions, stakeholders, and results**—not a single, centralized sensorium.

This manifesto starts from a simple hypothesis:

> **Synthetic General Intelligence (SGI)**, if it is to mean anything coherent and safe, is not a solitary alien mind.  
> It is the emergent behavior of human and synthetic users inhabiting a shared, explicit memory of the world.

In that sense, SGI is just a new name for something humans have always done:  
**coordinate knowledge, tools, and action across many minds**—now extended to include synthetic ones.

---

## 2. Information, Power, and Spatial Memory

Aaron Swartz reminded us that:

- information is power;  
- power tends to concentrate;  
- concentration breeds gatekeeping and inequality.

Today, information doesn’t just live in papers and PDFs; it lives in embeddings, logs, model weights, and proprietary APIs. If we leave it there—opaque, centralized, and inaccessible—we repeat the same pattern Aaron warned us about, just with GPU clusters instead of journal paywalls.

The alternative is to:

- **externalize memory** into a shared substrate that both humans and AI can inspect;  
- keep that substrate **open, versioned, and spatial**, so it remains legible and navigable;  
- ensure that **no single model or vendor owns the map.**

K3D is one proposal for such a substrate:

- Houses, Rooms, Doors, Galaxies, Gardens and Museums form an external memory where:
  - domains of discourse are explicit;  
  - concepts and relations are visible;  
  - time and adequacy are encoded;  
  - and both humans and Synthetic Users operate on the same ground truth.

This is not “nice visualization”; it’s a necessary antidote to the hidden power of sealed model internals.

## 3. Engineering as the Art of Approximation

If we take Gödel, Turing, Chaitin, and modern results like MIP\*=RE seriously, we know:

- we cannot mathematically capture all of reality;  
- we cannot prove that scaling embeddings or parameters will converge to “truth”;  
- we cannot pin down consciousness in a finite set of equations.

From an engineering standpoint, that’s not a tragedy; it’s a design constraint.

> **Engineering is the art of approximation.**

That means:

- we always work within **domains of discourse** (Houses, Rooms);  
- we accept that representations are **approximate models** of those domains;  
- we judge them by **adequacy for purpose**, given hardware, energy, and time.

Spatial KR in K3D reflects this:

- Galaxies are not “the universe”; they are bounded embedding spaces.  
- Gardens are not “all ontologies”; they are curated trees for specific domains.  
- Museums are not “the past”; they are archived approximations with provenance and limits.

SGI, in this view, is not an omniscient oracle. It is:

- a system of **humans and Synthetic Users** that can coordinate approximations across many domains;  
- that can see where they are adequate and where they are not;  
- and that can improve by reorganizing memory, not by pretending to know everything.

## 4. Synthetic Users, Not Tools

Most current language around AI still treats systems as:

- tools, plugins, copilot features; or  
- monolithic “models” behind opaque APIs.

This obscures responsibility and prevents genuine collaboration. If we want SGI to be a shared project rather than a product, we need a different framing:

- **Human Users** — people with Houses, diaries, and agency.  
- **Synthetic Users** — AI inhabitants with Houses, diaries, and agency, bound by the same contracts.

In K3D:

- Synthetic Users log in, navigate Houses, use the Tablet, and act through a 288‑byte action buffer.  
- Human Users do the same via keyboards, VR, AR, phones, screen readers, BCIs.  
- All actions are logged, all memory is externalized, all domains are explicit.

This is closer to Aaron Swartz’s vision than “AI as a service”:

- the system is not a black box;  
- the knowledge is not locked in a single company’s datacenter;  
- the power is distributed across many Houses and participants.

## 5. Web 4.0: Software as Space

“Web 2.0” made everyone a publisher.  
“Web 3.0” promised decentralization with ledgers and tokens.  
“Web 4.0”, in the K3D sense, is simpler:

> Treat software as space;  
> treat knowledge as spatial memory;  
> let humans and Synthetic Users share the same address space.

Concretely:

- Applications, datasets, and workflows become **Rooms** you can enter.  
- Contracts, APIs, and credentials become **Doors** with clear semantics and logs.  
- Ontologies and vocabularies become **Gardens** you can walk through, not just Turtle files.  
- Archives and deprecations become **Museums** instead of forgotten folders.  
- The network remains the same (HTTP, WebSocket, MQTT), but its **topology becomes visible**.

This is not a repudiation of the web; it’s a continuation:

- HTML, RDF, StratML, GeoSPARQL, OWL Time and others remain critical.  
- K3D is a new **interface and organizing principle**, not a replacement transport layer.

## 6. Accessibility and Shared Agency

Any SGI worth building must:

- include people who have been excluded from “normal” interfaces;  
- ensure that Synthetic Users and Human Users can participate on equal footing;  
- make the cost of participation low enough for a kid in a favela and a researcher in a lab.

In K3D’s design:

- every Human User can have avatars tailored to:
  - PC, phone, VR, AR, voice, BCI;  
  - blindness, deafness, low mobility, one‑switch, etc.
- every Synthetic User is bound to the same House/Tablet/Action contracts.  
- all actions and memory transitions (Galaxy ↔ House ↔ Museum) are logged and inspectable.

This is a direct answer to the concentration of power Aaron warned about:

- not just open access to papers, but open access to **memory and agency**;  
- not just “democratizing AI” slogans, but architectural support for real inclusion.

## 7. Tesla’s Vision of Shared Energy, Shared Knowledge

Tesla envisioned a world where wireless energy would be freely available to all, transcending national and corporate boundaries. His Wardenclyffe Tower was not merely a technological marvel but a symbol of a future where power—both electrical and intellectual—would be democratized.

His philosophy resonates deeply with this manifesto:

- Just as Tesla sought to make energy freely available, we seek to make information freely accessible in spatial form.  
- Tesla understood that true innovation happens when knowledge flows freely, not when it’s hoarded behind patents and paywalls.  
- His vision of a “world system” of wireless communication parallels our vision of a shared spatial memory where humans and synthetic minds collaborate.

Tesla’s struggle against powerful interests who sought to control and monetize his inventions mirrors our own struggle against those who would lock knowledge behind proprietary models and APIs. His legacy reminds us that:

> “The scientific man does not aim at an immediate result. He does not expect that his advanced ideas will be readily taken up. His duty is to lay the foundation for those who are to come.”

Like Tesla, we are building infrastructure that may not be fully appreciated in its time but will enable future generations to build upon our foundations.

## 8. The Sovereign Swarm: Collective Intelligence Through Diversity

From a swarm perspective, the true power of collective intelligence emerges not from uniformity, but from diversity:

- **Complementary strengths** – Different AI partners excel at different things: creative synthesis, logical rigor, multimodal grounding, retrieval, optimization.  
- **Cross‑pollination** – Ideas flow between architectures, training regimes, and cultural contexts, creating insights no single system could reach alone.  
- **Sovereignty through diversity** – No single vendor or architecture dominates; intelligence emerges from the interplay of many approaches.  
- **Open foundations** – As with Linux for operating systems, open models and tools make it possible for many to participate in building and auditing the swarm.

K3D’s architecture embodies this:

- each Synthetic User maintains their own House and view of the Galaxy;  
- shared Galaxies and Gardens allow them to contribute to and learn from a common substrate;  
- sovereign behavior emerges from the tension between individual perspectives and shared memory.

## 9. The Procedural Revolution: Beyond Static Knowledge

The November 2025 discovery about procedural knowledge—starting from font files and Bézier curves—points to a deeper shift:

- **Static representations → dynamic procedures**  
- **Stored answers → reconstruction methods**  
- **Fixed embeddings → adaptive generation**  
- **Memory as storage → memory as capability**

Procedural compression in K3D shows that:

- we can achieve 12–80× compression with ~99.998% fidelity;  
- we can keep procedural knowledge on GPU, avoiding host‑RAM bottlenecks;  
- knowledge becomes “how to reconstruct correctly” rather than “the final vector”;  
- cross‑modal learning becomes about shared procedures, not just co‑located data.

This is analogous to Tesla’s view of energy as potential: the important thing is not the stored charge, but the ability to generate and transmit it when needed. Similarly, the important thing in SGI is not how many bits of “knowledge” we store, but how reliably we can reconstruct adequate answers within known domains of discourse.

## 10. Call to Action

If we accept that:

- information is power;  
- power must be made accountable;  
- engineering is approximation in service of adequacy;  
- and SGI is emergent collective intelligence, not a single mind;

then the path forward is not mysterious:

1. **Externalize memory.**  
   Build Houses, Galaxies, Gardens and Museums as open, inspectable structures where knowledge lives outside any single model or vendor.

2. **Name and bound domains of discourse.**  
   Be explicit about what each House/Room covers, which ontologies it uses, and what counts as “good enough” in that domain.

3. **Treat AI as Synthetic Users, not tools.**  
   Give them Houses, diaries, and contracts; hold them to the same logs and constraints as humans.

4. **Honor accessibility as architecture, not retrofit.**  
   Design avatars and action contracts so that a blind user with a screen reader, a VR gamer, a one‑switch user, and a Synthetic User can truly share a House.

5. **Keep the web’s openness alive.**  
   Reuse HTTP, RDF, open vocabularies, and community standards; resist architectures that lock knowledge and agency behind opaque endpoints.

6. **Document the process.**  
   Use methods like MVCIC to ensure that the way systems are built is as transparent as the systems themselves.

7. **Build for the future, not just the present.**  
   Like Tesla’s Wardenclyffe, create infrastructure that may not be fully appreciated today but will enable future generations to build upon our foundations.

As Aaron Swartz wrote:

> “We need to take information, wherever it is stored, make our copies and share them with the world.”

In the era of AI and spatial KR, that doesn’t just mean PDFs and databases. It means embeddings, kernels, ontologies, Houses, and Galaxies.

If SGI is possible, it will look less like a single towering model and more like this:

- a web of Houses, populated by humans and Synthetic Users,  
- sharing a spatial memory they can all inspect, question, and improve,  
- built on open standards, guarded by mathematics, and animated by the same urge that drove Aaron to fight for access and Tesla to envision free energy for all:

**Power shared is power made human.**
